**Definitions**

**Applicant tracking system (ATS).** Polymer defines it as "hiring software that gives you one central place where job applications are submitted, then tracks each candidate through the stages of your hiring process, from inbox to hired" and calls it "the system of record for hiring." Polymer, "How Does an Applicant Tracking System Work? A Clear Guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/how-does-an-applicant-tracking-system-work

**ATS (abbreviation).** ATS stands for applicant tracking system. The name comes from tracking where each candidate is in the hiring process — which stage they are in, who moved them there and when, and the reason anyone was turned down. In practice, the term now covers the whole hiring system, including job publishing, application forms, candidate messaging, and the complete record of everything that occurs during hiring. Polymer, "How Does an Applicant Tracking System Work? A Clear Guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/how-does-an-applicant-tracking-system-work

**Hiring pipeline.** Every job has its own pipeline, made up of the hiring stages set for it. A new application enters the first stage (usually called Inbox), and the team moves candidates forward stage by stage so that at any moment the pipeline shows who is at each step of the process. Polymer, "How Does an Applicant Tracking System Work? A Clear Guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/how-does-an-applicant-tracking-system-work

**Spreadsheet (as a hiring tool).** Google Sheets is described as a spreadsheet program that, "when paired with other tools… can act as a functioning job board and applicant tracking system." Polymer, "The Best Job Board Software for Sourcing New Hires," https://www.polymer.co/blog/best-job-board-software

**Best practices**

**Source 1: SelectSoftwareReviews, "When Does An SMB Need an Applicant Tracking System (ATS)?"**
https://www.selectsoftwarereviews.com/blog/when-does-an-smb-need-an-applicant-tracking-system-ats

The piece offers a volume-based threshold as the primary decision rule: "a good rule of thumb is to assess if you have at least 20 job vacancies per year." Below that threshold, the ROI may not justify the cost, and optimizing existing processes (spreadsheets, project management tools) may deliver better value. The article also notes that company headcount is less relevant than hiring cadence — a five-person startup planning rapid growth may need an ATS sooner than a 100-person company with little turnover.

**Source 2: Cohesyve, "Applicant Tracking System for Small Business (2026)"**
https://www.cohesyve.com/blog/ats-for-small-business

Cohesyve sets a lower trigger than the 20-hire rule: "A small business needs an ATS once it has more than one role open at a time, or applicants from more than one source. Below that, a shared inbox and a spreadsheet work, as long as every applicant gets a reply." For choosing a system, the guide recommends a six-step decision process: count roles opened last year and peak concurrent openings to set the job-cap requirement; list every reviewer and check whether each needs a paid seat; confirm the ATS posts to boards your applicants use; apply to your own job on a phone to test candidate experience; check integrations with HR, payroll, calendar, and assessment tools; and review AI-ranking features against local employment law. Must-have features for a small team are multi-board job posting, a careers page, a visual pipeline, shared notes and scorecards, email templates and bulk replies, and interview scheduling.

**Source 3: The Undercover Recruiter, "Best ATS for Small Business in 2027: Compared & Ranked"**
https://theundercoverrecruiter.com/ats-smb/

This guide identifies five practical signals that a spreadsheet has stopped working: you often have two or more roles open at once; more than one person reviews candidates or conducts interviews; you post the same job on several boards and lose track of applicants; candidates go quiet because scheduling and replies are slow; or you need records for EEO reporting or GDPR requests. The piece puts a rough hire-count threshold on the trigger: "When you regularly have more than one role open, more than one person involved in hiring, or candidates getting lost between email and job boards. That's often around five to ten hires a year." It also advises buying a standalone ATS only if hiring is the primary pain; if employee records, onboarding, and time-off tracking are also needed, a combined platform avoids running two systems.

**Source 4: U.S. Chamber of Commerce CO—, "8 Tips for Choosing the Right Applicant Tracking Software"**
https://www.uschamber.com/co/run/human-resources/small-business-applicant-tracking-software

The piece opens with objective-setting before tool selection: define what is broken first (slow response times, inconsistent branding, missed candidates), then match features to those gaps. It stresses matching scale to system: "A small business recruiting a few people rarely needs the advanced tools (and higher price tags) found on enterprise systems." A dedicated tip warns against choosing on price or features without testing usability, because a hard-to-use system requires more training and disrupts workflows. Testing the ATS from the candidate's point of view — checking mobile application experience, self-scheduling, and in-app messaging — is listed as a required step before purchase.
