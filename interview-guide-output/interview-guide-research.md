**Definitions**

**Interview guide:** A document that enables organizations to structure how they conduct candidate interviews — covering the questions to ask and their order, evaluation criteria, scoring rubrics, interview logistics, and policies — so that every candidate for a role is assessed consistently. It "functions as a roadmap that goes beyond outlining interview questions, covering rules, policies, evaluation criteria, and the entire interview workflow from start to finish." Source: x0pa.com, *Interview Guide: Definition, Elements, Types & Benefits*, https://x0pa.com/glossary/interview-guide/

**Interview process:** The end-to-end sequence of stages through which a candidate is evaluated for a role, from initial screening through final decision. A structured interview process includes "an underlying job analysis and a standardized method for rating responses" in addition to the interview itself. Source: VidCruiter, *Structured Interviewing: Definition, Benefits, and Examples*, https://vidcruiter.com/interview/structured/

**Structured interview:** A hiring interview in which every candidate answers the same job-related questions in the same order, scored against the same predetermined rubric. The U.S. Office of Personnel Management defines it as "a standardized assessment method designed to evaluate candidates' job-related skills by systematically asking how they have responded in past experiences and how they would behave in hypothetical situations." Source: VidCruiter, *Structured Interviewing*, https://vidcruiter.com/interview/structured/

**Best practices**

**The Predictive Index — "Interview Best Practices for Hiring Managers"**
https://www.predictiveindex.com/blog/interview-best-practices-for-hiring-managers/

Covers ten practices for building a structured, consistent process. Key recommendations: build a scorecard before the interview starts to prevent gut-feel decisions; "assign specific competencies to each panelist so the interview covers real ground rather than covering the same ground multiple times"; follow the 80/20 rule — "aim for the candidate to carry roughly 80% of the conversation"; use behavioral questions rooted in real experience, since "'Tell me about a time when…' is more useful than 'What would you do if…'"; and dig deeper with follow-up probes when answers are vague or rehearsed, using questions such as "What specifically did you do in that situation?"

---

**Forbes — "Interviewing Etiquette 101: 15 Best Practices For Hiring Managers And Headhunters"**
https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbeshumanresourcescouncil/2023/01/04/interviewing-etiquette-101-15-best-practices-for-hiring-managers-and-headhunters/

Focuses on the candidate experience and the interviewer's conduct. Recommends sharing an interview itinerary with candidates beforehand so there are "minimal surprises"; being honest about the role, both positives and negatives; respecting the candidate's time and avoiding marathon multi-round days; and treating candidates like clients — "keep them engaged and answer any and all of their questions promptly." Also flags cultural differences in global hiring, recommending including an interviewer familiar with the candidate's cultural customs.

---

**Goldbeck Recruiting — "The Job Interview Process: Structure, Stages, Best Practices"**
https://goldbeck.com/blog/the-job-interview-process-structure-stages-and-best-practices/

Maps the interview process end to end, from a short screening call (15–30 minutes by phone or video) through structured first rounds to final conversations. Best practices for structure include "using the same core questions for all candidates, scoring responses against defined criteria, separating 'must-have' from 'nice-to-have' skills, training interviewers on evaluation, not persuasion." Notes that behavioral questions "tend to be more predictive of future performance," while situational questions help when candidates lack direct experience, and "a strong interview process usually blends both." Common mistakes listed: too many rounds without added value, different interviewers assessing the same thing, and overreliance on gut feel.

---

**Paradigm IE — "Interviewing Best Practices: Master a Defensible Hiring Process"**
https://www.paradigmie.com/post/interviewing-best-practices

Emphasizes legal defensibility and bias reduction. Recommends running a structured debrief: "Before anyone speaks, have each interviewer silently finalize their scores on the scorecard" to prevent groupthink, then review one competency at a time so each person shares evidence. Trained interviewers write objective notes — for example, "instead of a subjective note like 'good communicator,' a trained interviewer writes, 'Candidate clearly explained a complex project using the STAR method.'" Documentation to keep: completed scorecards, behavioral notes, and a written summary of the final decision rationale.
