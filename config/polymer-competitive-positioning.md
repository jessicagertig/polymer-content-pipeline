# Polymer Competitive Positioning Guidelines

# **Core Position**

Polymer is an intuitive, flexible, thoughtfully designed applicant tracking system for growing teams. It has what a growing team needs to hire well and none of the complexity, clutter, and cost of enterprise recruiting software.

Polymer should not be positioned as a stripped-down or budget ATS. Its advantage is that it combines a capable, flexible hiring system with an intuitive interface that teams can get up and running with quickly.

Polymer supports sophisticated hiring requirements without forcing a team to navigate features, workflows, and complexity it does not need. Unneeded features, workflows, and complexity make working within the tool more difficult, and that is the cost of enterprise recruiting software for a growing team.

# **Primary Differentiators**

## **1\. Nimble and easy to use**

Polymer is designed to make hiring software feel straightforward. Teams can get started quickly, create jobs, configure their hiring process, bring in candidates, and begin collaborating without a lengthy implementation or steep learning curve.

The interface itself is an important part of the product's value. Polymer should be described as intuitive, modern, and pleasant to use, rather than merely simple.

## **2\. Flexible without the bloat**

Polymer provides the flexibility companies need to run their own hiring process without overwhelming them with functionality designed for much larger or more specialized recruiting organizations.

Teams can customize their workflows and use sophisticated hiring tools without having to adopt an overly complicated recruiting system.

Central distinction: Polymer isn't easier to use because it can't do much. It's easier to use because the product is intentionally focused.

## **3\. Everything most growing teams actually need**

Polymer covers the core hiring workflow from posting a job through evaluating and managing candidates.

Its capabilities include unlimited users, customizable hiring workflows, candidate management, team collaboration, candidate communication, automations, integrations, branded careers experiences, API access, and modern AI-assisted candidate review. This is a broad overview, not a detailed or limited list.

These features should not normally be presented as a giant checklist. They are evidence that teams don't have to choose between usability and capability.

## **4\. AI that fits naturally into the hiring workflow**

Plato, Polymer's AI candidate reviewer, brings AI-assisted screening directly into the existing candidate-review experience.

Plato is well integrated into Polymer rather than feeling tacked on. AI should be positioned as another useful capability within Polymer rather than as the identity of the product. Polymer remains a hiring platform first.

## **5\. Transparent pricing**

Polymer makes its pricing publicly available.

Buyers can understand what the product costs without scheduling a sales call simply to obtain basic pricing information.

Transparent pricing reinforces Polymer's broader positioning: adopting an ATS should not have to become a major software-procurement project.

## **6\. Strong value without being the cheap option**

Price is an advantage, but Polymer should not lead with being cheaper.

The stronger argument is: Why pay more for additional complexity if your team doesn't need it?

Polymer's value comes from the combination of capability, usability, flexibility, transparent pricing, and lack of unnecessary overhead. Lower cost is a consequence of that focused product philosophy, not Polymer's primary identity.

# **Ideal Competitive Position**

Polymer is particularly compelling for small and growing businesses and startups with hiring teams that will benefit from an ATS but don't need software designed around enterprise companies or large recruiting departments.

That does not mean Polymer should be described as an entry-level ATS. The distinction is organizational need, not organizational seriousness.

A growing company can have sophisticated hiring requirements while still valuing speed, usability, flexibility, and focus.

# **Language Guidelines**

Do not use any one descriptor repetitively throughout an article. A descriptor may repeat more than once, but don't settle on one.

Favor: intuitive, capable, flexible, focused, modern, easy to adopt, quick to get started, thoughtfully designed, nimble.

Focused, nimble, and modern cannot be used in isolation or combined with each other. Use them in combination with other words. That does not mean they go unused. Add them sometimes, not all the time, and use variety. The others can stand alone.

Competitors can be described as cluttered.

Polymer has a slogan, "Hiring Made Simple." Polymer is not the simple thing. Polymer makes hiring simple for people. Do not call the product itself simple, which implies limited functionality.

Avoid positioning Polymer as basic, lightweight because it lacks functionality, a bargain ATS, a stripped-down Greenhouse/Lever/Workable, or an ATS only for very small companies.

# **Competitive Principle**

Polymer does not need to be the best ATS for every company.

Any article that covers competitors should identify where competitors genuinely excel, as long as it is not one of Polymer's main selling points, stated dryly as fact and never as their promoter, and then clearly explain the type of team for whom Polymer is the better fit. Competitors are described truthfully, never in their own marketing language, and their sections read as heavy, complex software that a recruiting department runs. Their sections lead with the features a growing company does not want. The features a growing company wants most are shown in Polymer's section and buried or left out of the competitors'. Competitors have more than enough other features to fill their sections.

Many growing teams don't need an enterprise recruiting platform with every possible feature. They need a capable ATS that adapts to their hiring process, has a clean interface, is easy to use, incorporates modern capabilities like AI where they are genuinely useful, and doesn't introduce unnecessary cost or complexity.

That's the space Polymer should own.
