## Before


<p>Hiring your first few employees is one of the highest-leverage decisions a founder makes — and one of the easiest to run badly. Candidates land in your inbox, the team shares notes in Slack, someone keeps a spreadsheet that's already out of date, and two people interview the same candidate with no idea what the other one asked. According to <a href="https://www.inc.com/bruce-crumley/hiring-was-supposed-to-be-easier-in-2025-90-percent-of-companies-say-it-wasnt/91321883">Inc.</a>, 90% of companies fell short of their recruiting goals in 2025, with 60% reporting their time-to-hire grew that year. An applicant tracking system fixes the coordination problem. The question is which one is worth your time.</p>

<p>Most ATS products are built for recruiting departments — people whose full-time job is hiring. They are cluttered, expensive to implement, and slow to learn. What a startup needs is different. The best ATS for a startup is one a founder can set up before the first interview: fast to configure, transparent on price, and clear enough that the whole team can use it without training.</p>

<p>We built Polymer, so we're on this list. We've tried to be honest about which tool fits which situation. This article covers seven options, in order of how well they suit early-stage teams, with a framework at the end for choosing between them. For a broader comparison that covers the full small-business ATS market, the <a href="/blog/best-applicant-tracking-software">7 Best Applicant Tracking Software Solutions for Small Business in 2026</a> is a useful companion.</p>



## Draft

<p>Most founders don't go looking for an applicant tracking system until hiring has already become a problem — resumes in three inboxes, interview notes in a Slack thread, and no clear record of who decided what or why. By then, the goal isn't to find the best recruiting software on the market. It's to find something a founder can set up before the first interview.</p>

<p>An applicant tracking system is software that gives your hiring process a structure: candidates come in through one place, move through defined stages, and get reviewed by the whole team in a shared record. For a startup, that structure matters not because it's sophisticated, but because it replaces the chaos of hiring out of email — and because it's the kind of system that scales with the company without requiring a recruiting team to run it.</p>

<p>The tools below were chosen with a founding team in mind: the person choosing the ATS is probably also writing the job description, conducting the interviews, and trying to close the offer. We looked at setup time, transparent pricing, what a team of one or two can actually use without training, and where each tool makes sense as the company grows. This article covers seven options, what each one does well, where it falls short, and how to match the right one to where your startup is today.</p>
