Most founders don't go looking for an applicant tracking system until hiring has already become a problem — resumes scattered across inboxes, interview notes buried in Slack, and no shared record of who said what about whom. The best ATS for a startup is the one a founder can set up before the first interview.

The right ATS for your startup depends on where you are in the company's life and who is doing the hiring. If you're making your first three hires without a recruiter, you need a tool that's fast to configure and easy to use without training — a tool built for a founder, not an enterprise platform built for a recruiting department you don't have yet. Some tools on this list are built for teams at that stage, and others are built for the stage after it, when you have a head of people and a pipeline to manage.

This article covers seven applicant tracking systems chosen for founding teams — what each one does well, where it falls short, and how to match the right one to where your startup is today.

## Polymer

Polymer is an applicant tracking system built for growing teams. It covers the full hiring workflow — posting jobs, managing candidates through a customizable pipeline, communicating with applicants, collaborating with the hiring team, and running AI-assisted screening — without the complexity that enterprise tools carry for recruiting departments a startup doesn't have. For a founder who needs to publish a role, review applications, and schedule interviews without spending a week on setup, that focus is the product.

Every plan includes unlimited users, unlimited candidates, a branded job board, customizable hiring stages, candidate messaging with templates and automations, scored candidate reviews, and Plato, Polymer's AI candidate reviewer. Plato scores each application against the job's own requirements before anyone has to open a resume. Pricing is published: Starter is $124/month billed annually, Growth is $233/month, and Scale is $415/month. Plans differ by how many jobs you can publish at once and how many Plato credits you receive each month. The free trial starts when you publish your first job post, and you don't need a credit card before that point — which means you can build out your hiring stages, add your branding, and preview the board before committing to anything.

Plato removes name, location, and email from each application before scoring, so the first read on every candidate is based on what they've done. Each review gives every requirement in the job description a verdict — clear evidence, related evidence, or no evidence — and assigns the candidate an overall fit level from Excellent to Poor, with the reasoning shown. That makes it easier to triage a large applicant pool quickly, which matters when the person reviewing applications is also the person running the company.

Polymer connects to Slack, Discord, Zapier, and Webflow. Jobs publish to Google Jobs automatically and to LinkedIn through the LinkedIn Limited Listings integration. The whole team can review candidates, leave comments, and score interviews from the same place, with no additional seats to purchase. For more on how the product fits into a startup's hiring process end to end, the [applicant tracking for startups](/applicant-tracking-for-startups) page covers the full picture.

**Best for:** Founding teams and early-stage startups that want a capable, thoughtfully designed ATS without a long implementation or a cluttered interface. If the person setting it up is also the person interviewing candidates, Polymer is built for exactly that situation.

**Pro:** Fast to set up, transparent pricing, AI screening included on every plan, and the whole team can be in the tool without paying per seat.

**Con:** Polymer is built around active hiring. It does not include a dedicated CRM for building passive talent pipelines over time — a limitation for teams that want to cultivate candidate relationships ahead of a growth phase.

## Breezy HR

Breezy HR is a mid-market ATS with a permanent free plan — one active job position, no time limit — that makes it one of the few tools a startup can try before spending anything. The Bootstrap plan covers basic candidate tracking and a careers page. It costs nothing, and for a startup making its very first hire, that is worth considering before paying for a trial elsewhere.

The Startup plan at $157/month (billed annually) removes the job cap and adds one-way video screening, automated workflows, and candidate questionnaires. The Growth plan at $273/month adds interview scorecards, advanced analytics, and employee referrals. All plans include unlimited users. Breezy posts to 50+ job boards in one step, launches a branded careers site in under a minute with no coding required, and handles candidate self-scheduling so applicants can book their own interviews without back-and-forth email. Its Breezy Intelligence add-on brings AI applicant scoring, resume fraud detection, and an AI notetaker to the platform.

The free plan's single-job cap is real. The moment you're hiring for two roles at once — which happens fast at a startup that starts growing — you need the paid tier. That said, the upgrade path is clear: the Startup plan is reasonably priced, and the feature set grows predictably from there.

**Best for:** Early-stage startups that want to start for free and grow into more features as hiring volume increases, or teams where candidate self-scheduling is a priority and back-and-forth scheduling is a genuine drain on time.

**Pro:** The free plan is functional for a single open role, with no credit card or sales call required to get started.

**Con:** The single-job cap on the free plan becomes a constraint the moment a second role opens, pushing teams to the paid tier earlier than expected.

## JazzHR

JazzHR prices by active job posting, not by user or by employee headcount — a structure that works in a startup's favor when the whole team needs access but the number of open roles is small. The Hero plan is roughly $75/month billed annually and covers up to three active jobs, which is enough for most early-stage teams. Unlimited users are included on every plan.

JazzHR posts to dozens of free and paid job boards in one step, including LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, and ZipRecruiter. Its TalentFit AI surfaces the strongest candidates from the applicant pool, and its interview scheduling tool syncs with Google Calendar and Outlook. The Plus plan at roughly $269/month (billed annually) adds custom workflows and team collaboration; the Pro plan at roughly $420/month adds offers, eSignatures, and API access. A 21-day free trial is available without a credit card.

JazzHR has been in the market long enough to have deep integrations and a large customer base, which translates into thorough documentation — useful when the person setting it up has no IT support. The per-job pricing model also means that growing the team doesn't automatically grow the bill; costs are tied to hiring activity, not company size.

**Best for:** Startups hiring for one to three roles at a time that want broad job board distribution and prefer costs tied to what they're actively hiring for, rather than how big the company has grown.

**Pro:** Per-job pricing keeps costs predictable when the hiring team is large but open roles are few.

**Con:** The lowest plan caps at three active jobs. If you're in a growth phase with more than three searches running simultaneously, costs climb quickly and the per-job model loses its appeal.

## Workable

Workable is a full-featured ATS with published pricing, broad job board distribution, and AI sourcing built in — a combination that makes it one of the more capable mid-market options a startup can evaluate without a sales call. The Standard plan starts at $299/month billed annually for companies with 1–20 employees.

Workable's AI sourcing tool proactively surfaces candidates from a database of 400 million+ profiles, with written match reasoning for each recommendation. It posts to 200+ job boards from a single dashboard and includes built-in interview scheduling with calendar integration. The platform also covers HR features beyond hiring — onboarding, time off, and employee records — which can reduce tool sprawl for a startup that is building out its HR stack at the same time it's building its team.

The price scales with employee headcount, not jobs. At 21–50 employees, the Standard plan rises to $419/month billed annually; at 51–100 employees, it reaches $669/month. That is worth modeling before signing an annual contract: a startup that grows from 15 to 35 employees midway through the year will see a price jump regardless of whether the number of open roles has changed. Workable also offers a free trial.

**Best for:** Startups that want proactive sourcing baked in, need a large job board network, or are building out an HR layer alongside their recruiting function and want one platform to cover both.

**Pro:** AI sourcing, wide distribution, and published pricing make Workable straightforward to evaluate. The HR feature set means fewer tools to manage as the team grows.

**Con:** Headcount-based pricing means costs rise as the company grows, even if hiring volume stays flat. The feature set is broader than most early-stage founding teams need or will use.

## Ashby

Ashby combines an ATS, a recruiting CRM, interview scheduling, and analytics into one platform. That consolidation is its central argument: teams that would otherwise stitch together three or four separate tools can run everything through Ashby. The Foundations plan starts at around $300–$400/month for companies with 1–10 employees — verify the current figure directly at ashbyhq.com/pricing, as published figures have varied by source — and scales with company size from there. The Plus tier for companies over 100 employees is quote-only.

Ashby's analytics are the most sophisticated on this list for a growing company: custom dashboards, pipeline conversion tracking, and diversity reporting are built in rather than added on. Its workflow automation tools let teams build structured, repeatable hiring processes across multiple roles and departments — the kind of infrastructure that makes hiring a scalable operational function rather than a reactive scramble.

That depth comes with setup time. Ashby is designed for teams that want to invest in configuring a system, not for founders who need to post a role before the week is out. There is no published free trial, which makes the entry experience more involved than any other tool on this list. For a high-growth startup — typically Series A and beyond — with a dedicated recruiting hire who treats sourcing and hiring analytics as core responsibilities, that investment pays off. For a founding team doing its first three hires, it is usually too much to take on.

**Best for:** High-growth startups with a dedicated recruiting function that is building hiring infrastructure to support dozens of hires per year and wants a single platform to replace multiple tools.

**Pro:** The consolidated platform eliminates tool sprawl, and the built-in analytics give recruiting teams visibility that most ATS products don't approach.

**Con:** The setup complexity and the absence of a published free trial make Ashby a difficult first ATS for a founder who needs to hire two people and return to building the product.

## Greenhouse

Greenhouse is a heavyweight enterprise hiring platform with the price tag and implementation requirements to match. It does not publish its prices. Independent sources citing buyer-reported data, including [Best Recruiting Tools](https://bestrecruitingtools.com/blog/greenhouse-ats-review-2026), place the entry-level Core tier at roughly $5,000 to $12,000 per year for companies under 150 employees, with one-time implementation fees that range from $1,000 to $15,000 or more. The Plus and Pro tiers go significantly higher. That is not a startup price — it is a price for a company that is spending enough on recruiting to justify a heavyweight system.

What Greenhouse offers at that price is an industry-leading structured hiring system: scorecards and interview kits that standardize evaluation across the team, DEI reporting with anonymized resume review, sourcing automation, and over 500 integrations with other tools. For a recruiting department that is accountable for filling dozens of roles per quarter with consistent, defensible process, that infrastructure is genuine value.

Greenhouse earns its place on this list because a small number of startups genuinely need it — specifically, those that have raised a significant round, hired a head of people or a full recruiting team, and are building hiring infrastructure designed to serve hundreds of employees. For every other startup on this list, the cost and implementation overhead will outweigh the features.

**Best for:** Well-funded startups with a dedicated people team that is building enterprise-grade hiring infrastructure from the first day and can absorb both the cost and the implementation investment.

**Pro:** Industry-leading structured hiring tools, deep integrations, and enterprise-grade DEI and compliance reporting.

**Con:** No published pricing, high entry costs, and an implementation process that requires significant time and often external support. It is too much tool for a founding team.

## Lever

Lever is a combined ATS and recruiting CRM built around the idea that passive and active candidates should live in one place. Its NurtureKit feature handles outreach to passive candidates over time — sequences, follow-ups, and pipeline tracking for people who aren't looking today but might be in six months. For a startup that is proactively building a talent bench ahead of a growth phase, that capability is genuinely useful.

Lever does not publish its prices. Buyer-reported data from procurement tracking, cited by [Hire Truffle](https://www.hiretruffle.com/blog/lever-ats) across 318 reported purchases, puts the median negotiated contract at around $15,400 per year, with a range from roughly $6,700 for small teams to over $51,900 for larger ones. Annual contracts are required. The platform moved to a single-plan structure in 2026, with AI screening available as a paid add-on.

For a startup doing primarily inbound, reactive hiring — a role opens, the team reviews applicants, someone gets hired — the CRM depth in Lever is overhead rather than value. The annual contract and the opacity around pricing add friction that most founding teams would rather avoid. Lever is best suited to companies that have already crossed into a recruiting function, where someone's job is to build and maintain candidate relationships over time.

**Best for:** Growth-stage startups with a recruiting function that is proactively sourcing and nurturing passive candidates, and where a combined ATS and CRM in one place reduces the need for separate tools.

**Pro:** The combined ATS and CRM means passive and active candidates share one record, with a full relationship history that survives from first contact to eventual hire.

**Con:** No published pricing, annual contracts required, and the CRM depth becomes unnecessary overhead for a startup doing primarily inbound hiring at the early stage.

## How to Choose the Right ATS for Your Startup

The right tool depends on where you are in the company's life and who is doing the hiring. A founding team making its first three hires has different needs than a 60-person company with a head of people running 15 searches at once. These four questions narrow the field.

### Who is running the hiring process?

If the founder, a hiring manager, or a generalist is running hiring without dedicated recruiting support, the ATS has to be fast to set up and easy to use without training. Polymer, Breezy HR, and JazzHR are built for that situation. Ashby, Greenhouse, and Lever assume someone will invest real time in configuration — and ongoing management of a more complex system.

### Which pricing model matches your growth curve?

ATS tools price in meaningfully different ways. Per-job pricing, like JazzHR's, keeps costs predictable when the team grows but hiring volume stays low. Headcount-based pricing, like Workable's, can jump mid-contract when the company scales even if no new roles are open. Quote-only pricing, like Greenhouse's and Lever's, means you cannot evaluate the full cost without a sales conversation — friction that slows a decision a startup needs to make quickly. Per-job and transparent pricing tend to serve early-stage teams better.

### Do you need a recruiting CRM?

Most early-stage startups do not. They are hiring reactively: a role opens, they fill it, and they move on. CRM features — like Lever's NurtureKit or Ashby's sourcing tools — deliver real value when a startup is proactively building a talent pipeline ahead of a growth phase. If your team isn't doing that yet, you're paying for capability you won't reach for.

### What does the next funding round change?

Some founding teams choose their first ATS knowing they'll outgrow it. That is a reasonable call — switching tools at 50 employees is a manageable project, and paying for Greenhouse at 10 employees is usually a poor trade. If a single platform for the long term is the goal, Ashby is the most capable of the mid-market options at the entry price point, and Workable adds an HR layer that can serve the company well past the startup stage.

## Start Simple, Upgrade When the Complexity Earns Its Keep

The best ATS for a startup is the one that gets candidates into a structured pipeline quickly, keeps the whole team in the same place, and doesn't require a recruiting department to run. Most founding teams overestimate what they need at the beginning and underestimate how much simpler hiring becomes once candidates stop living in email threads and spreadsheets.

Start with a tool you can configure and use in a day. Build your hiring stages, write your job posts, and get the first few hires through a consistent process. The habits you build in those first rounds of hiring will matter more than the features on a comparison chart.

When the tool starts to slow you down — when you're hiring enough people that reporting matters, or when you want to build candidate pipelines in advance — that's the right moment to move up. By then, you'll know exactly what you need.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an ATS, and does a startup actually need one?

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is hiring software that gives a team one central place to post jobs, receive applications, and track each candidate through the hiring process. Startups need one earlier than most founders expect — typically around the second or third hire, when coordinating interviews and feedback across two or three people becomes genuinely difficult without a shared system.

### How much does an ATS cost for a startup?

ATS pricing for startups ranges from free (Breezy HR's one-job Bootstrap plan) to roughly $75–$300 per month for mid-market tools like JazzHR, Polymer, and Workable at the entry level. Enterprise platforms like Greenhouse and Lever don't publish prices and typically start in the $5,000–$15,000 per year range, making them a poor fit for most early-stage teams.

### Can a founder set up an ATS without an HR or IT team?

Yes. Tools like Polymer, Breezy HR, and JazzHR are designed to be configured by the person doing the hiring, not by an implementation specialist. Most founding teams can go from sign-up to a live job post in under an hour. Tools like Ashby and Greenhouse require significantly more setup time and are better suited to teams with a dedicated recruiting hire.

### What features matter most for a startup's first ATS?

The features that matter most at the early stage are a customizable hiring pipeline, candidate messaging, team collaboration without per-seat costs, and job board distribution. AI-assisted screening is increasingly useful even at small scale — it helps a founder triage a large applicant pool without spending hours on initial review.

### When should a startup switch to a more powerful ATS?

The right time to move to a more sophisticated platform is when your current tool starts limiting what your recruiting function can do — typically when you're making 20 or more hires per year, when reporting and analytics have become important for accountability, or when you want to build proactive talent pipelines rather than hiring purely from inbound applications.

### Is Polymer a good ATS for a startup?

Polymer is built for growing teams that want a capable, flexible hiring system without the complexity and cost of enterprise software. Every plan includes unlimited users, AI-assisted candidate screening, customizable hiring stages, and published pricing — which are the features most founding teams need most. It's designed to be set up quickly by the person doing the hiring, without a dedicated recruiter or IT support.
