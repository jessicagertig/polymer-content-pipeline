# The Best ATS for Startups in 2026: 7 Tools Compared for Founding Teams

Most founders don't go looking for an applicant tracking system until hiring has already become a problem — resumes scattered across inboxes, interview notes buried in Slack, and no shared record of who said what about whom. The best ATS for a startup is the one a founder can set up before the first interview.

The right ATS for your startup depends on where you are in the company's life and who is doing the hiring. If you're making your first three hires without a recruiter, you need a tool that's fast to configure and easy to use without training — a tool built for a founder, not an enterprise platform built for a recruiting department you don't have yet. Some tools on this list are built for teams at that stage, and others are built for the stage after it, when you have a head of people and a pipeline to manage.

This article covers seven applicant tracking systems chosen for founding teams — what each one does well, where it falls short, and how to match the right one to where your startup is today.

## Polymer

Polymer is an applicant tracking system built for teams that are growing and hiring now. It covers the full hiring workflow — posting jobs, managing candidates through a customizable pipeline, communicating with applicants, collaborating with the hiring team, and running AI-assisted screening — without the complexity that enterprise tools carry for recruiting departments a startup doesn't have. A founder can publish a role, review applications, and schedule interviews on the same day the account is created.

Those features are available on every plan: unlimited users, unlimited candidates, a branded job board, customizable hiring stages, candidate messaging with templates and automations, scored candidate reviews, and Plato, Polymer's AI candidate reviewer. Plato scores each application against the job's own requirements before anyone has to open a resume. Pricing is published on Polymer's pricing page. Plans differ by how many jobs you can publish at once and how many Plato credits you receive each month. The free trial starts when you publish your first job post, and you don't need a credit card before that point, which means you can build out your hiring stages, add your branding, and preview the board before committing to anything.

Plato is also the system's equity layer — it removes name, location, and email from each application before scoring, so the first read on every candidate is based on what they've done. Each review gives every requirement in the job description a verdict — clear evidence, related evidence, or no evidence — and assigns the candidate an overall fit level from Excellent to Poor, with the reasoning shown. That makes it easier to triage a large applicant pool quickly, which matters when the person reviewing applications is also the person running the company.

Beyond screening, the platform handles distribution. Jobs publish to Google Jobs automatically and to LinkedIn through the LinkedIn Limited Listings integration. Polymer also connects to Slack, Discord, Zapier, and Webflow for teams that use those tools. The whole team can review candidates, leave comments, and score interviews from the same place, with no additional seats to purchase. For more on how the product fits into a startup's hiring process end to end, the [applicant tracking for startups](/applicant-tracking-for-startups) page covers the full picture.

**Best for:** Founding teams and early-stage startups that want an ATS with a clean interface and a setup measured in hours, not weeks. If the person setting it up is also the person interviewing candidates, Polymer is built for exactly that situation.

**Pro:** Ready to use quickly, transparent pricing, AI screening included on every plan, and the whole team can be in the tool without paying per seat.

**Con:** Polymer is built around active hiring. It does not include a dedicated CRM for building passive talent pipelines over time, a limitation for teams that want to cultivate candidate relationships ahead of a growth phase.

## Breezy HR

Breezy HR is a mid-market ATS with a permanent free plan — one active job position, no time limit — that makes it one of the few tools a startup can try before spending anything. The Bootstrap plan covers basic candidate tracking and a careers page at no cost, which means a startup making its first hire can get started without committing to a paid trial.

All plans include unlimited users, which matters because a startup's hiring team rarely maps to a tidy seat count. Breezy posts to 50+ job boards in one step, launches a branded careers site in under a minute with no coding required, and handles candidate self-scheduling so applicants can book their own interviews without back-and-forth email. The Breezy Intelligence add-on extends those capabilities with AI applicant scoring, resume fraud detection, and an AI notetaker.

That free plan has a hard limit of one active role at a time, so the moment a startup opens a second role simultaneously, the paid tier is required. The paid tiers add features in defined layers: the Startup plan removes the job cap and adds one-way video screening, automated workflows, and candidate questionnaires, while the Growth plan adds interview scorecards, advanced analytics, and employee referrals.

**Best for:** Early-stage startups that want to begin hiring at no cost and scale into more features as volume grows, or teams that want candidates to book their own interviews without scheduling back-and-forth.

**Pro:** The free plan is functional for a single open role, with no credit card or sales call required to get started.

**Con:** The single-job cap on the free plan becomes a constraint the moment a second role opens, pushing teams to the paid tier earlier than expected.

## JazzHR

JazzHR prices by active job posting, not by user or by employee headcount — a structure that works in a startup's favor when the whole team needs access but the number of open roles is small. Its entry-level plan covers up to three active jobs. Unlimited users are included on every plan.

On distribution, JazzHR posts to dozens of free and paid job boards in one step, including LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, and ZipRecruiter. Its TalentFit AI ranks candidates by fit and its interview scheduling tool syncs with Google Calendar and Outlook, so the team can move from application to booked interview without switching tools. Higher-tier plans add custom workflows, team collaboration, offers, eSignatures, and API access, all of which become relevant once the hiring process involves more than a single reviewer. A 21-day free trial is available without a credit card.

JazzHR's documentation is thorough enough to cover configuration without IT support, which matters for a founder working without a dedicated recruiter. The per-job pricing model also means that growing the team doesn't automatically grow the bill; costs are tied to hiring activity, not company size.

**Best for:** Startups hiring for one to three roles at a time that want broad job board distribution and prefer costs tied to what they're actively hiring for, rather than how big the company has grown.

**Pro:** Per-job pricing keeps costs predictable when the hiring team is large but open roles are few.

**Con:** The lowest plan caps at three active jobs. If you're in a growth phase with more than three searches running simultaneously, costs climb quickly and the per-job model loses its appeal.

## Workable

Workable is a full-featured ATS with broad job board distribution and AI sourcing built in. Pricing scales with employee headcount, and because pricing is published, the platform is straightforward to evaluate without a sales call.

That sourcing tool searches a profile database and surfaces candidates with written match reasoning, so a founder can see the rationale behind each result. The platform posts to a broad network of job boards from a single dashboard and includes built-in interview scheduling with calendar integration. Beyond hiring, it covers onboarding, time off, and employee records, which means fewer tools to manage as the team grows.

One trade-off worth modeling before signing an annual contract is the headcount-based pricing. A startup that grows from 15 to 35 employees midway through the year will see a price jump regardless of whether the number of open roles has changed. Workable also offers a free trial.

**Best for:** Startups that want proactive sourcing baked in, need a large job board network, or are building an HR layer alongside their recruiting function and want one platform to cover both.

**Pro:** Published pricing and a wide job board network make Workable straightforward to evaluate. Because the platform covers onboarding, time off, and employee records alongside hiring, a startup building its HR stack at the same time it is building its team has fewer tools to manage as it grows.

**Con:** Headcount-based pricing means costs rise as the company grows, even if hiring volume stays flat. Early-stage startups pay for capabilities they are not yet using.

## Ashby

Ashby combines an ATS, a recruiting CRM, interview scheduling, and analytics into one platform. That consolidation is its central argument: teams that would otherwise stitch together three or four separate tools can run everything through Ashby. Ashby does not publish its prices. The Foundations plan for companies with 1–10 employees is quote-only, and the Plus tier for companies over 100 employees is also priced on a sales call, so contact Ashby directly for a current figure.

That consolidation extends to reporting. Ashby's analytics are the most sophisticated on this list for a growing company. Custom dashboards, pipeline conversion tracking, and diversity reporting are built in rather than added on. Its workflow automation tools let teams build structured, repeatable hiring processes across multiple roles and departments, the kind that turns hiring into a scalable operational function rather than a role-by-role scramble.

That analytical depth requires real configuration time. Ashby is designed for teams that want to invest in configuring a system, not for founders who need to post a role before the week is out. It has no published free trial, so a team that wants to evaluate it before committing must go through a sales process. For a high-growth startup, typically Series A and beyond, with a dedicated recruiting hire who treats sourcing and hiring analytics as core responsibilities, that work is well spent. For a founding team doing its first three hires, it is usually too much to take on.

**Best for:** High-growth startups with a dedicated recruiting function that is building hiring infrastructure to support dozens of hires per year and wants a single platform to replace multiple tools.

**Pro:** The consolidated platform eliminates tool sprawl. The built-in analytics give recruiting teams visibility that few ATS products match.

**Con:** The setup complexity and the absence of a published free trial make Ashby a difficult first ATS for a founder who needs to hire two people and return to building the product.

## Greenhouse

Greenhouse is a heavyweight enterprise hiring platform with the price tag and implementation requirements to match. It does not publish its prices, and costs depend on company size and are set in a sales call. That is not a startup price. It is what a company pays when it is spending enough on recruiting to justify a heavyweight system.

What Greenhouse offers at that price is an industry-leading structured hiring system: scorecards and interview kits that standardize evaluation across the team, DEI reporting with anonymized resume review, sourcing automation, and over 500 integrations with other tools. For a recruiting department that is accountable for filling dozens of roles per quarter with consistent, defensible process, that infrastructure is genuine value.

Even so, Greenhouse earns its place on this list because a small number of startups genuinely need it — specifically those that have raised a significant round, hired a head of people or a full recruiting team, and are building hiring infrastructure designed to scale to hundreds of employees. For every other startup on this list, the cost and implementation overhead will outweigh the features.

**Best for:** Well-funded startups with a dedicated people team that is building enterprise-grade hiring infrastructure from day one and can absorb both the cost and the implementation investment.

**Pro:** Industry-leading structured hiring tools, deep integrations, and enterprise-grade DEI and compliance reporting. These tools give a recruiting department defensible, consistent evaluation across every role.

**Con:** No published pricing, high entry costs, and an implementation process that requires significant time and often external support — more than a founding team at the early stage can justify.

## Lever

Lever is a combined ATS and recruiting CRM built around the idea that passive and active candidates should live in one place. Its NurtureKit feature handles outreach to passive candidates over time — sequences, follow-ups, and pipeline tracking for people who aren't looking today but might be in six months. For a startup that is proactively building a talent bench ahead of a growth phase, that capability lets one team manage both sourcing and hiring without switching tools.

Lever's pricing reflects its positioning as a combined ATS and CRM built for teams with active sourcing programs. Lever does not publish its prices. [Buyer-reported data across 318 purchases](https://www.hiretruffle.com/blog/lever-ats) puts the median negotiated contract at around $15,400 per year, with a range from roughly $6,700 for small teams to over $51,900 for larger ones, and the actual price depends on company size and is set in a sales call. Annual contracts are required. The platform moved to a single-plan structure in 2026, with AI screening available as a paid add-on.

That depth is the trade-off. For a startup doing primarily inbound, reactive hiring, where a role opens, the team reviews applicants, and someone gets hired, the CRM depth in Lever is overhead rather than value. The annual contract requirement and the absence of published pricing make it harder for a founding team to evaluate the cost before a sales call. Lever is best suited to companies that have already crossed into a recruiting function, where someone's job is to build and maintain candidate relationships over time.

**Best for:** Growth-stage startups with a recruiting function that is proactively sourcing and nurturing passive candidates, and where a combined ATS and CRM in one place reduces the need for separate tools.

**Pro:** The combined ATS and CRM means passive and active candidates share one record, with a full relationship history that survives from first contact to eventual hire.

**Con:** No published pricing, annual contracts required, and the CRM depth becomes unnecessary overhead for a startup doing primarily inbound hiring at the early stage.

## How to Choose the Right ATS for Your Startup

A founding team making its first three hires has different needs than a 60-person company with a head of people running 15 searches at once. These questions narrow the field.

### Who is running the hiring process?

If the founder, a hiring manager, or a generalist is running hiring without dedicated recruiting support, the ATS has to be fast to set up and easy to use without training. Polymer, Breezy HR, and JazzHR are built for that situation. Ashby, Greenhouse, and Lever assume someone will invest real time in configuration and ongoing management of a more complex system.

### Which pricing model matches your growth curve?

Not every ATS bills on the same model, and the differences matter. Some tools charge per open role, which keeps costs predictable when the team grows but hiring volume stays low. Others charge based on headcount, which can jump mid-contract when the company scales even if no new roles are open. Some enterprise tools do not publish prices and require a sales conversation before you can evaluate the full cost, a delay that adds friction at a stage when a startup needs to make decisions quickly. Per-job and transparent pricing tend to serve early-stage teams better.

### Do you need a recruiting CRM?

Most early-stage startups do not. They are hiring reactively: a role opens, they fill it, and they move on. CRM features — like Lever's NurtureKit or Ashby's sourcing tools — deliver real value when a startup is proactively building a talent pipeline ahead of a growth phase. Teams that are not yet doing that are paying for capability they will not use.

### What does the next funding round change?

Some founding teams choose their first ATS knowing they'll outgrow it. Starting with a lighter tool is a reasonable approach. Switching early is far less painful than carrying the cost and configuration overhead of an enterprise-tier platform that rarely pays off at 10 employees. If a single platform for the long term is the goal, Ashby is the most capable of the mid-market options at the entry price point, and Workable adds an HR layer that can serve the company well past the startup stage.

## Start Simple, Upgrade When the Complexity Earns Its Keep

The best ATS for a startup is the one that gets candidates into a structured pipeline quickly, keeps the whole team in the same place, and works without a recruiting department. Most founding teams overestimate what they need at the beginning and underestimate how much simpler hiring becomes once candidates stop living in email threads and spreadsheets.

Start with a tool you can configure and use in a day. Build your hiring stages, write your job posts, and get the first few hires through a consistent process. The habits you build in those first rounds of hiring will shape how your team evaluates and moves candidates — and a consistent evaluation process produces fairer, more defensible hiring decisions than any feature on a comparison chart.

The process compounds over time. When the tool starts to slow you down — when you're hiring enough people that reporting matters, or when you want to build candidate pipelines in advance — that's the right moment to move up. By then, you'll know exactly what you need.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an ATS, and does a startup actually need one?

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is hiring software that gives a team one central place to post jobs, receive applications, and track each candidate through the hiring process. Startups need one earlier than most founders expect — typically around the second or third hire, when coordinating interviews and feedback across two or three people becomes genuinely difficult without a shared system.

### How much does an ATS cost for a startup?

ATS pricing for startups ranges from free (Breezy HR's one-job Bootstrap plan) to roughly $75–$300 per month for mid-market tools like JazzHR, Polymer, and Workable at the entry level. Enterprise platforms like Greenhouse and Lever don't publish prices and typically start in the $5,000–$15,000 per year range, making them a poor fit for most early-stage teams.

### Can a founder set up an ATS without an HR or IT team?

Yes. Tools like Polymer, Breezy HR, and JazzHR are designed to be configured by the person doing the hiring, not by an implementation specialist. Most founding teams can go from sign-up to a live job post in under an hour. Tools like Ashby and Greenhouse require significantly more setup time and are better suited to teams with a dedicated recruiting hire.

### What features matter most for a startup's first ATS?

The features that matter most at the early stage are a customizable hiring pipeline, candidate messaging, team collaboration without per-seat costs, and job board distribution. AI-assisted screening is increasingly useful even at small scale — it helps a founder triage a large applicant pool without spending hours on initial review.

### When should a startup switch to a more powerful ATS?

The right time to move to a more sophisticated platform is when your current tool starts limiting what your recruiting function can do — typically when you're making 20 or more hires per year, when reporting and analytics have become important for accountability, or when you want to build proactive talent pipelines rather than hiring purely from inbound applications.

### Is Polymer a good ATS for a startup?

Polymer is built for growing teams that want a capable, flexible hiring system without the complexity and cost of enterprise software. Every plan includes unlimited users, AI-assisted candidate screening, customizable hiring stages, and published pricing — which are the features most founding teams need most. It's designed to be set up quickly by the person doing the hiring, without a dedicated recruiter or IT support.
