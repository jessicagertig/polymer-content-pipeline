<h2>Polymer</h2>
<p>Polymer is an applicant tracking system built for growing teams. It covers the full hiring workflow — posting jobs, managing candidates through a customizable pipeline, communicating with applicants, collaborating with the hiring team, and running AI-assisted screening — without the complexity that enterprise tools carry for recruiting departments a startup doesn't have. For a founder who needs to publish a role, review applications, and schedule interviews without spending a week on setup, that focus is the product.</p>

<p>Every plan includes unlimited users, unlimited candidates, a branded job board, customizable hiring stages, candidate messaging with templates and automations, scored candidate reviews, and Plato, Polymer's AI candidate reviewer. Plato scores each application against the job's own requirements before anyone has to open a resume. Pricing is published: Starter is $124/month billed annually, Growth is $233/month, and Scale is $415/month. Plans differ by how many jobs you can publish at once and how many Plato credits you receive each month. The free trial starts when you publish your first job post, and you don't need a credit card before that point — which means you can build out your hiring stages, add your branding, and preview the board before committing to anything.</p>

<p>Plato removes name, location, and email from each application before scoring, so the first read on every candidate is based on what they've done. Each review gives every requirement in the job description a verdict — clear evidence, related evidence, or no evidence — and assigns the candidate an overall fit level from Excellent to Poor, with the reasoning shown. That makes it easier to triage a large applicant pool quickly, which matters when the person reviewing applications is also the person running the company.</p>

<p>Polymer connects to Slack, Discord, Zapier, and Webflow. Jobs publish to Google Jobs automatically and to LinkedIn through the LinkedIn Limited Listings integration. The whole team can review candidates, leave comments, and score interviews from the same place, with no additional seats to purchase. For more on how the product fits into a startup's hiring process end to end, the <a href="/applicant-tracking-for-startups">applicant tracking for startups</a> page covers the full picture.</p>

<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Founding teams and early-stage startups that want a capable, thoughtfully designed ATS without a long implementation or a cluttered interface. If the person setting it up is also the person interviewing candidates, Polymer is built for exactly that situation.</p>

<p><strong>Pro:</strong> Fast to set up, transparent pricing, AI screening included on every plan, and the whole team can be in the tool without paying per seat.</p>

<p><strong>Con:</strong> Polymer is built around active hiring. It does not include a dedicated CRM for building passive talent pipelines over time — a limitation for teams that want to cultivate candidate relationships ahead of a growth phase.</p>

<p>JazzHR prices by active job posting, not by user or by employee headcount — a structure that works in a startup's favor when the whole team needs access but the number of open roles is small. A 21-day free trial is available without a credit card.</p>

<p>JazzHR has been in the market long enough to have deep integrations and a large customer base, which translates into thorough documentation — useful when the person setting it up has no IT support. The per-job pricing model also means that growing the team doesn't automatically grow the bill; costs are tied to hiring activity, not company size.</p>

<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Startups hiring for one to three roles at a time that want broad job board distribution and prefer costs tied to what they're actively hiring for, rather than how big the company has grown.</p>

<p><strong>Pro:</strong> Per-job pricing keeps costs predictable when the hiring team is large but open roles are few.</p>

<p><strong>Con:</strong> The lowest plan caps at three active jobs. If you're in a growth phase with more than three searches running simultaneously, costs climb quickly and the per-job model loses its appeal.</p>

<h2>How to Choose the Right ATS for Your Startup</h2>
<p>The right tool depends on where you are in the company's life and who is doing the hiring. A founding team making its first three hires has different needs than a 60-person company with a head of people running 15 searches at once. These four questions narrow the field.</p>

<h3>Who is running the hiring process?</h3>
<p>If the founder, a hiring manager, or a generalist is running hiring without dedicated recruiting support, the ATS has to be fast to set up and easy to use without training. Polymer, Breezy HR, and JazzHR are built for that situation. Ashby, Greenhouse, and Lever assume someone will invest real time in configuration — and ongoing management of a more complex system.</p>

<h3>Which pricing model matches your growth curve?</h3>
<p>ATS tools price in meaningfully different ways. Per-job pricing, like JazzHR's, keeps costs predictable when the team grows but hiring volume stays low. Headcount-based pricing, like Workable's, can jump mid-contract when the company scales even if no new roles are open. Quote-only pricing, like Greenhouse's and Lever's, means you cannot evaluate the full cost without a sales conversation — friction that slows a decision a startup needs to make quickly. Per-job and transparent pricing tend to serve early-stage teams better.</p>

<h3>Do you need a recruiting CRM?</h3>
<p>Most early-stage startups do not. They are hiring reactively: a role opens, they fill it, and they move on. CRM features — like Lever's NurtureKit or Ashby's sourcing tools — deliver real value when a startup is proactively building a talent pipeline ahead of a growth phase. If your team isn't doing that yet, you're paying for capability you won't reach for.</p>

<h3>What does the next funding round change?</h3>
<p>Some founding teams choose their first ATS knowing they'll outgrow it. That is a reasonable call — switching tools at 50 employees is a manageable project, and paying for Greenhouse at 10 employees is usually a poor trade. If a single platform for the long term is the goal, Ashby is the most capable of the mid-market options at the entry price point, and Workable adds an HR layer that can serve the company well past the startup stage.</p>
