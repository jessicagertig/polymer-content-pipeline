## Opening

### As written

<p>Most hiring teams don't go looking for two tools when one seems to cover the job. If you've been told you need both an applicant tracking system and a recruiting CRM — or if you're trying to figure out which one you actually need — the difference matters more than the naming implies. The two tools address different parts of the hiring process, and running the wrong one for your situation creates real gaps: either a process with no way to reach candidates who haven't applied, or a relationship strategy with no way to close a hire.</p>

<p>An applicant tracking system manages active hiring — the pipeline from application to offer, with structured stages, team collaboration, and a compliance record of every step. A recruiting CRM manages relationships with people who haven't applied yet: passive candidates, past finalists, contacts made at events, and anyone else worth staying in touch with before a role opens. The ATS works the pipeline. The CRM fills it.</p>

<p>For most growing companies and startups, the question isn't which tool wins — it's which one you need right now, and whether there's a point at which you need both. This article defines both tools, compares them directly, explains the gaps that appear when you run only one, and gives clear guidance on who needs an ATS, who needs a CRM, and when it makes sense to run both.</p>

## Final

<p>Most hiring teams use a single tool for the whole hiring process, though managing an active pipeline and building candidate relationships before anyone applies call for different workflows and serve different purposes. An applicant tracking system manages active hiring — the pipeline from application to offer, with structured stages, team collaboration, and a compliance record of every step.</p>

<p>A recruiting CRM manages relationships with candidates who haven't applied yet — passive candidates, past finalists, contacts made at events, and anyone else a team wants to keep in contact with before a role opens. The CRM fills the pipeline. The ATS moves candidates through it, from application to offer.</p>

<p>Both functions need to be covered. For most growing companies and startups, the decision is whether to handle both in a single tool or to give each a dedicated one.</p>

<p>This article defines both, compares them, and shows who needs an ATS, who needs a CRM, and when both make sense.</p>
