## Definitions

**Hiring process**
Polymer does not publish a standalone definition of "the hiring process" as a topic. The closest description from Polymer's own content comes from its ATS article, which describes the hiring process as a sequence of stages — from inbox to hired — behind every open job, run by a hiring team and tracked in a central system. Source: Polymer, "How Does an Applicant Tracking System Work? A Clear Guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/how-does-an-applicant-tracking-system-work

---

**Recruitment process**
Polymer defines recruitment as "the process of filling vacant positions at your company," one that is "leveraged to attract and hire positions at speed" and that "often follows a simple workflow: Outline the role — Advertise it and collect applications — Screen applications — Invite people to interview — Decide who to hire." Recruitment addresses a company's short-term hiring needs; it refers specifically to "screening, assessing, and hiring new employees." Source: Polymer, "Talent Acquisition vs. Recruitment: Which is Most Important?" https://www.polymer.co/blog/talent-acquisition-vs-recruitment

---

**Talent acquisition**
Polymer defines talent acquisition as "a long-term human resources strategy for hiring the best and most relevant people for your organization," in which "the hiring process is driven by business growth and success" rather than simply filling a vacancy. It is distinguished from recruitment in that it is "strategic and involves long-term planning about who to hire," while recruitment is "a short-term, practical solution to fill open positions." Talent acquisition includes creating a hiring strategy, identifying top talent, relationship building, planning sourcing channels, building employer brand, building talent pipelines, succession planning, and data analysis. Source: Polymer, "What is talent acquisition? A complete guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/talent-acquisition and "Talent Acquisition vs. Recruitment: Which is Most Important?" https://www.polymer.co/blog/talent-acquisition-vs-recruitment

---

**Hiring pipeline**
Polymer describes the hiring pipeline as the set of stages every job has, through which candidates move from application to hired or archived. "Every job has its own pipeline, made up of the hiring stages you set for it. A new application goes into the first stage, usually called Inbox, and the candidate appears in that stage's list. From there, the team moves candidates forward stage by stage, so at any moment the pipeline shows who is at each step of the process." Source: Polymer, "How Does an Applicant Tracking System Work? A Clear Guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/how-does-an-applicant-tracking-system-work

---

**Recruitment methods**
Polymer does not define "recruitment methods" as a standalone term. Within the talent acquisition process, Polymer describes candidate sourcing methods as including social media platforms such as LinkedIn, job sites and job boards, reviewing past applications, and employee referrals. Source: Polymer, "What is talent acquisition? A complete guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/talent-acquisition

---

**Agile recruiting process**
Polymer defines the agile recruiting process as "a talent acquisition framework based on the principles of the agile methodology, a model first developed in 2001 by a team of software developers." For recruiters, it means "bringing greater flexibility to the hiring process" by breaking "recruitment campaigns into digestible phases" that are "prioritized and completed by targeted deadlines." Source: Polymer, "Agile Recruiting Process: 5 Steps to Implement It," https://www.polymer.co/blog/agile-recruiting-process

---

**Skills mapping**
Polymer defines skills mapping as "a methodical process that identifies and evaluates the specific abilities required for a job. Instead of relying on job titles or years of experience, it focuses on the actual skills necessary to perform tasks. By doing so, it creates a data-driven approach to assessing candidates, ensuring their qualifications align with the demands of the role." Source: Polymer, "Skills Mapping for Hiring: A Complete Guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/skills-mapping-for-hiring-a-complete-guide

---

**Behavioral interview scoring matrix**
Polymer defines the behavioral interview scoring matrix as "a recruitment framework that measures how candidates behave and respond to different situations. Candidates draw from their experience to answer a series of competency-based questions. Their answers show how they've performed in the past, their skills, and whether they'd be a good fit for the role." It is also called a hiring matrix. Source: Polymer, "Behavioral Interview Scoring Matrix: A Template & Simple Guide," https://www.polymer.co/blog/behavioral-interview-scoring-matrix

**Best practices**

**AIHR, "Selection Process: 7 Steps, Criteria & Tools To Hire Top Talent," https://www.aihr.com/blog/selection-process-practical-guide/**

AIHR recommends standardizing the selection process end to end. Its checklist includes "reliable or automated screening methods, structured interview formats, use of interview guides, consistently applied assessments, established criteria and scorecards for evaluating candidates, thorough documentation and checklists." On the intake side, AIHR advises recruiters to speak directly with hiring managers rather than rely only on a written form: "recruiters who practice active listening will glean valuable details on what personality types and soft skills suit the role." For interviews, AIHR recommends the STAR method tied to key competencies, plus an interview evaluation form to keep scoring consistent. It also calls for training anyone involved in decisions on structured interviewing, unconscious bias awareness, and how to interpret candidate behavior.

**Wow Remote Teams, "Hiring Process Steps: Complete Guide to Better Hires," https://wowremoteteams.com/glossary-term/hiring-process/**

This guide opens with the principle that "the hiring process should begin with the work that needs an owner, not with a job title," and advises defining the business problem before choosing what kind of person solves it. On interview structure, it warns that "not every hire needs five interviews" and that adding rounds collecting duplicate information creates delay without producing better evidence. Its troubleshooting table is a strong practical tool: for example, when "interviewers disagree constantly," the fix is "use structured questions and scorecards"; when "candidates withdraw," the fix is "shorten stages and communicate timing." On process improvement, it recommends measuring each transition point—"role approval → search begins → first qualified candidates → interviews → decision → offer → acceptance → start"—to find where days actually accumulate, rather than concluding generically that hiring takes too long.

**Testask, "Hiring process best practices: proven steps for better talent," https://testask.org/blog/hiring-process-best-practices-proven-steps-for-better-talent/**

Testask organizes its guidance around five principles. First, align criteria before sourcing: ask the hiring manager to "describe outcomes, not tasks," rank must-have versus nice-to-have skills so the team can make consistent trade-offs, and set a target offer date, because "vague timelines cause bottlenecks and candidate drop-off." Second, prioritize skills over credentials, and share evaluation criteria with candidates upfront: "candidates perform better and feel more respected when they understand what is being measured and why." Third, structure interviews: derive every question from a job-analysis competency, mix behavioral and situational formats, have each interviewer submit scores independently before the debrief, and "calibrate your rubric regularly" because a consistently high or low scorer is a calibration problem. Fourth, source strategically: track source-of-hire by quality, not volume—"a channel that produces 50 applicants but one good hire is less valuable than one that produces 10 applicants and three." Fifth, treat candidate experience as a differentiator: small gestures, like having the hiring manager (not HR) deliver the offer, can set an organization apart.

**business.com, "How to Create a Successful Hiring Process," https://www.business.com/articles/hiring-process-timeline/**

business.com advises companies to "establish standards and systems for every stage of the process, from determining your hiring needs through sourcing, screening, interviewing, selecting and onboarding," quoting a practitioner: "when you have clear standards and steps in place for the team to follow, there is less risk of confusion or need for discussion as you move through the process." On screening volume, it recommends interviewing three to five candidates in a first round rather than five to ten, to avoid wasting time on candidates who do not meet minimum qualifications.

**Statistics**

1. **41 days** — Average U.S. time to fill an open role in 2024, down from 48 days in 2023 (a full 7-day improvement). Source: SHRM (Recruiter Nation Report benchmarking data, cited by SHRM). Publication: SHRM. Article: "Recruiters Express Optimism for 2025." Year: 2024. URL: https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/recruiters-express-optimism-for-2025

2. **78%** — Share of organizations using pre-employment assessments that say those assessments improved the quality of their hires. Source: SHRM. Publication: SHRM. Article: "SHRM 2024 Talent Trends Report." Year: 2024. URL: https://www.shrm.org/content/dam/en/shrm/research/2024-talent-trends-research-overall-findings.pdf

3. **73%** — Share of organizations that eliminated degree requirements for certain positions and reported finding at least one new hire they would have previously considered unqualified. Source: SHRM. Publication: SHRM. Article: "Designing a No-Fuss Skills-Based Hiring Process." Year: 2024. URL: https://www.shrm.org/enterprise-solutions/insights/designing-no-fuss-skills-based-hiring-process

4. **Two in three** (66%) — Share of employees hired in the last year who reported that their candidate experience with their current company was "exceptional" (27%) or "very good" (39%), indicating strong experiences precede accepted offers. Source: Gallup. Publication: Gallup. Article: "The Lasting Impact of Exceptional Candidate Experiences." Year: 2024. URL: https://www.gallup.com/workplace/651650/lasting-impact-exceptional-candidate-experiences.aspx

5. **85%** of organizations say technology use improved candidates' experience (up from 78% in 2022 and 68% in 2021); **82%** say it sped up the recruitment process (up from 72% in 2022). Source: CIPD. Publication: CIPD. Article: "Resourcing and Talent Planning Report 2024." Year: 2024. URL: https://www.cipd.org/globalassets/media/knowledge/knowledge-hub/reports/2024-pdfs/8662-resource-and-talent-planning-2024-report-web.pdf

6. **Twice as predictive** — Structured interviews are twice as predictive of job performance as unstructured ones. Source: Research cited in SHRM vendor directory. Publication: SHRM. Article: "The Definitive Guide to Structured Interviewing." Year: not dated. URL: https://vendordirectory.shrm.org/company/354019/whitepapers/11772/the-definitive-guide-to-structured-interviewing *(Note: treat with moderate confidence; the underlying research is widely cited but this page does not name an independent study author.)*

7. **30% higher profitability** — Organizations that focus on hiring the top 20% of candidates typically achieve 30% higher profitability, per Gallup research cited by SHRM. Source: Gallup (as cited). Publication: SHRM. Article: "How to Audit Your Internal Hiring Practices for Bias." Year: not dated. URL: https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/news/how-to-audit-your-internal-hiring-practices-for-bias *(Note: the original Gallup page for this statistic was not independently verified in this search.)*
