Some combine an ATS with a CRM for teams doing outbound sourcing. Some put hiring inside a broader HR or workforce platform. And some are capable, flexible hiring products for growing companies that want a solid ATS without the overhead.

## Is Ashby the right fit for your team?

### Where Ashby is strongest

Ashby is an all-in-one recruiting platform that consolidates an ATS, sourcing and candidate relationship management, interview scheduling, and analytics into a single system. It's built for a recruiting team that runs structured hiring at scale, with deep funnel analytics, conversion tracking, and dashboard reporting on every plan. Scheduling is native, so a recruiting coordinator can book a multi-stage panel from inside the same tool that tracks the candidate. AI features span the platform: an AI Candidate Assistant for sourcing outreach, AI-assisted application review, and an AI Notetaker as a paid add-on.

**Choose Ashby if** you have a recruiting team that will use the CRM and analytics, you're hiring across multiple roles in parallel, and you want all of that in one contract.

### How Ashby is priced

Ashby prices by total company headcount, not per recruiter seat. The All-In-One Foundations plan covers companies up to 100 employees on a published calculator: $300 per month for 1 to 10 employees, $400 for 11 to 25, $500 for 26 to 50, $700 for 51 to 75, and $900 for 76 to 100. A 10% discount applies on annual commitments. All-In-One Plus, for 101 to 1,000 employees, is quote only. All-In-One Enterprise, for companies above 1,000, is also quote only. [Vendr reports](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/ashby) a median buyer paying $20,939 per year across 59 deals.

### What's included and what's added on

Every All-In-One plan includes the ATS, sourcing and CRM, scheduling, and analytics together. AI credits are included at no additional cost on every plan; additional credits cost $500 per year for 5,000. The AI Notetaker is a paid add-on on every plan — pricing is not published. There is no published free trial, and no free plan is confirmed on Ashby's pricing page.

### Who tends to look elsewhere

Ashby's pricing is based on total company headcount, which means the bill rises as the company grows whether or not hiring volume changes. A startup that added ten people last quarter pays more for its ATS this quarter, even if it has the same number of roles open. That model works for a stable organization but can feel misaligned for a fast-growing one.

Teams also look elsewhere when they don't need everything Ashby bundles. The CRM, the scheduling module, and the analytics layer are genuinely powerful, but a company where hiring managers post roles and review applicants — without a dedicated recruiting team running outreach — may not use most of them. Paying for a platform built around a recruiting operation you don't have creates overhead without a return. If either of those is your situation, the seven tools below cover the range of alternatives, from an easy-to-adopt ATS for growing companies to enterprise suites for global hiring.

## 1. Polymer: Best for growing companies

[Polymer](https://www.polymer.co/) is a modern, intuitive ATS for growing companies, from a founder interviewing their first hires to a company with hiring managers and a recruiter. Users are unlimited, so anyone you want involved in hiring gets a seat. Plans are tiered by how many jobs you need open at once, not by how many people you employ.

### Why consider it

You want a complete, modern applicant tracking system you can start using today, with a branded careers page, easy team collaboration, and built-in AI candidate screening — without weeks of onboarding. Polymer covers the full hiring workflow, including custom application forms, automations, job distribution, and an API, but skips the administrative weight that comes with enterprise recruiting software.

Polymer is intentionally focused, and that's what makes it easy to adopt. It supports sophisticated hiring requirements without forcing your team to navigate workflows and complexity it doesn't need. A growing company can have serious hiring standards while still valuing speed, usability, and flexibility. For a deeper look at how an ATS works under the hood, the article on [how an applicant tracking system works](/blog/how-does-an-applicant-tracking-system-work) covers the mechanics.

### Key features

* **Message automations and templates.** Hiring stage automations send a templated message to every candidate who enters a stage — no manual follow-up needed. Message templates with placeholders for the candidate's name, your name, the job title, and your organization make every communication repeatable across roles.
* **Plato AI screening.** Plato reviews each applicant against the requirements in the job description you already wrote, assigning each one a fit level and evidence-based reasoning across every criterion. Filter the pipeline by fit score and start with your strongest candidates.
* **Candidate reviews and review requests.** Any team member can submit a scored review or request one from a colleague — even someone outside the hiring team — by sending a Review kit with the candidate's profile. The whole team's feedback is visible in one place on the candidate record.
* **Company job board.** A branded careers page is set up at signup, ready to go on your own domain, in the language your candidates read, with job videos, category groupings, and social share images. Customizable application forms add multiple question formats, file uploads, and Admin-only fields per job.
* **Customizations.** A customer API manages candidates and job data on every plan. Notifications go to Slack or Discord, a Google Analytics stream ID tracks board traffic, Zapier triggers connect hiring events to your other tools, automatic posting to your LinkedIn company page, and a fully custom hiring pipeline round out the configuration options.

### Pricing

Polymer publishes its [pricing](https://www.polymer.co/pricing). Starter is $124 per month, Growth is $233, and Scale is $415, each billed annually. Month to month, they're $149, $279, and $499. Every plan has the complete feature set, unlimited users, and unlimited candidates. The plans differ by how many jobs you can have published at once — 5, 20, or 50 — and by monthly Plato credits: 50, 100, or 150.

You can create jobs, build the board, and add your team before you enter a payment method. A 14-day free trial starts when you publish your first job, and you can switch plans during the trial without ending it.

### Where it beats Ashby

Polymer's plans are priced by how many jobs you need open at once, not by how many people work at your company. Ashby's Foundations plan prices by total company headcount — $300 to $900 per month depending on the band — so the bill climbs as the company grows whether or not hiring is picking up. A startup that doubled its team this year but kept the same two open roles pays more than it did before. With Polymer, what drives the cost is hiring activity, not company size.

Setup is the other advantage. Polymer is running the same day you sign up. The job board, hiring stages, and message templates are ready out of the box, and a job needs only a title and a description to publish. Plato AI turns on against that description with no configuration required. Customize anything you like while applications keep arriving. Ashby is powerful, but it was built for a recruiting team to configure and administer, and the setup reflects that.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby's native scheduling and analytics are purpose-built for a recruiting team running structured hiring at volume. Interview scheduling, funnel conversion tracking, and CRM-style outreach are part of the same platform and share one candidate record. Polymer doesn't have a sourcing CRM or native scheduling. Ashby also includes sourcing tooling on every plan, so a team doing outbound recruiting doesn't need a separate tool.

### Best fit

**Choose Polymer if** you're a growing company that wants a capable, flexible ATS that's ready to use the day you sign up. If you have a dedicated recruiting team running outbound sourcing and funnel analytics, Ashby is built for that.

## 2. Greenhouse: Best for enterprise recruiting teams

[Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/) is an applicant tracking system built for a talent acquisition department. If your company has recruiters, recruiting coordinators, and a head of TA who reports hiring metrics to leadership, this is the system that team would run.

### Why consider it

You need an enterprise-focused recruiting platform, and you have a dedicated team to administer it. Before a role opens, the team defines competencies, writes an interview plan, assigns each interviewer a kit of questions, and sets the scorecard rubric. Interviewers submit scorecards before the debrief, so the panel enters the room with scores already on the table. The article on [Greenhouse alternatives](/blog/greenhouse-alternatives) covers other enterprise-tier options if you're evaluating at that level.

### Key features

* **Interview plans and kits.** Each stage of each role gets a plan with assigned interviewers, focus competencies, interview questions, and a scorecard rubric.
* **Requisition and offer approval chains.** Roles open only after a configured approval chain signs off, and offers go through their own chain before a candidate sees one.
* **Integrations marketplace.** Greenhouse connects to 500+ partners for assessments, background checks, scheduling, HRIS sync, and sourcing tools, including MCP support for external AI platforms.
* **Reporting.** Pipeline, source, time-to-fill, offer acceptance, and diversity reports are built in, with an AI-powered report builder on higher tiers.
* **Greenhouse AI.** An AI voice interviewer runs first-round phone screens, a notetaker maps transcripts to scorecard questions, AI drafts interview questions and scorecard attributes, and a resume review feature redacts identifying details before scoring. Advanced features, including AI-powered report filters, are on higher tiers.

### Pricing

Greenhouse doesn't publish pricing. Plans are Core, Plus, and Pro, priced by company headcount on custom quotes. [Independent reviewers](https://bestrecruitingtools.com/blog/greenhouse-ats-review-2026) report Core at roughly $5,000 to $12,000 per year for companies under 150 employees, Plus at $15,000 to $35,000 for companies of 150 to 1,000, and Pro at $40,000 or more. [Roles.at](https://roles.at/compare/greenhouse-pricing), citing verified contracts, puts the range at $10,627 to $75,900 per year with a median around $27,000. Expect a sales cycle, an annual contract, and an implementation that typically takes four to eight weeks.

### Where it beats Ashby

Greenhouse has a wider integration ecosystem than Ashby, with 500+ partner connections for assessments, background checks, and HRIS sync that a large talent acquisition team needs. Its structured hiring tooling — interview kits tied to competencies, scorecards submitted before the debrief, and AI-drafted questions — is built to run the same process across dozens of hiring managers in parallel. The fraud-detection capability (Real Talent) flags fraudulent applications before they reach a recruiter.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby publishes its pricing for companies up to 100 employees and includes scheduling, CRM, and analytics on every plan without add-ons or a separate contract. Greenhouse prices by quote only, requires an implementation project before the first job posts, and treats sourcing and scheduling as separate pieces to assemble. Ashby's analytics layer is deeper for a team that tracks funnel conversion, and its CRM outreach tooling runs from the same record as the ATS. Greenhouse is a larger implementation, built for a larger organization.

### Best fit

**Choose Greenhouse if** you have a recruiting team running structured, scorecard-driven hiring across many roles, a budget for a multi-week implementation, and a need for the widest possible integration ecosystem. If your managers hire for their own openings, look at Polymer.

## 3. Lever: Best for teams that source passive candidates

[Lever](https://www.lever.co/) is an ATS and recruiting CRM in a single database. It's built for a recruiting team that spends its days sourcing people who haven't applied and keeping them warm until a role opens.

### Why consider it

You want a full recruiting CRM alongside your ATS, and recruiters on your team are doing outbound work. They source passive candidates, tag them, put them into nurture sequences, and log every touchpoint over months before a role opens. When the right opening comes up, the contact's record becomes an application without starting over.

### Key features

* **LeverTRM (ATS + CRM).** The applicant pipeline and the sourcing pipeline share one candidate record, so a passively nurtured contact and a direct applicant are never duplicated.
* **NurtureKit.** Multi-step automated outreach sequences keep passive candidates warm over months, with tracking on opens and replies and exit rules for when someone responds.
* **LinkedIn Recruiter System Connect.** A two-way sync with LinkedIn Recruiter puts InMail activity and profile data on the Lever record for teams that pay for LinkedIn Recruiter.
* **Candidate Loss Risk alerts.** AI flags candidates showing signs of fading engagement — slowing response times, reduced activity — so a recruiter can step in before an offer is declined.
* **Generative AI.** AI features include Talent Fit scoring, interview transcripts and summaries, Fraud Signals, and an optional AI Screening add-on powered by VONQ.

### Pricing

Lever doesn't publish pricing. As of 2026, one unified plan is priced by company headcount on a custom quote, with Candidate Insights, AI Screening by VONQ, and Onboarding as separately priced add-ons. [HireTruffle](https://www.hiretruffle.com/blog/lever-ats), citing Vendr's 318 reported purchases, puts the median negotiated contract at $15,399 per year, with a range of $6,714 to $51,864. [Compono](https://www.compono.com/articles/lever-pricing-2026-real-cost-breakdown) reports $6 to $8 per employee per month. No free trial is available, and an annual contract is required.

### Where it beats Ashby

Lever's NurtureKit gives recruiters a dedicated outreach tool that Ashby's CRM doesn't match for depth. Multi-step sequences with open and reply tracking, exit rules, and a full touchpoint log mean a recruiter can run a passive pipeline for months and pick up exactly where the last conversation ended when a role opens. The LinkedIn Recruiter System Connect integration is also tighter than what Ashby offers, putting InMail history directly on the Lever record.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby publishes its Foundations pricing and includes scheduling and analytics on every plan. Lever prices by quote only and requires an annual contract with no free trial. Ashby's funnel analytics and conversion tracking are deeper than Lever's reporting. Teams looking for a combined recruiting CRM and analytics platform in one contract often find Ashby easier to compare and evaluate before signing.

### Best fit

**Choose Lever if** you have recruiters whose job is sourcing passive candidates and running nurture campaigns over months. If your candidates come from inbound applications, the CRM layer goes mostly unused.

## 4. Workable: Best for teams that want sourcing and HR from one vendor

[Workable](https://www.workable.com/) is a widely used applicant tracking system with a sourcing database and an optional HR suite. It's built for a company that wants recruiting and, increasingly, HR from one vendor.

### Why consider it

You want a sourcing database your recruiting team can search alongside an ATS, and you'd like to consolidate hiring and HR under the same platform over time. Workable's AI sourcing runs against more than 400 million profiles, so a recruiter can find candidates who haven't applied. The HR suite — employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation — is an add-on, available when the company is ready for it.

### Key features

* **AI sourcing database.** Workable's sourcing layer searches a database of 400+ million candidate profiles with AI on top, so a recruiter can run outbound search from inside the ATS.
* **Broad job board distribution.** Jobs post to many job boards from inside Workable, with tracking on which sources produce applicants.
* **HR suite.** Employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation are available as a separate add-on for companies that want their ATS and HR software from the same vendor.
* **Video interviews and assessments.** Native video interviewing and candidate assessments are available on Premier and Enterprise plans; add-ons on Standard.
* **Workable Agent.** A paid AI add-on on every plan that evaluates candidates against an ideal profile, sources passively, and powers a candidate chat. Credits are sold in packs starting at $600 for 5,000; every paid account gets 3,000 to start.

### Pricing

Workable [publishes its pricing](https://www.workable.com/pricing). Plans are priced by total company headcount. For a company of 1 to 20 employees billed annually, Standard is $299 per month, Premier is $599, and Enterprise is $719. Prices rise with headcount. Standard is available month to month; Premier and Enterprise require annual commitments. Workable HR is a separate add-on at $99 per month on annual billing for companies of 1 to 20. A 15-day free trial is available.

### Where it beats Ashby

Workable publishes its prices and bills Standard month to month with no minimum term, so a team can try it without an annual commitment. Ashby's Foundations plan is also published for companies up to 100 employees, but Plus and Enterprise are quote only. Workable's sourcing database — over 400 million profiles — is one of the larger ones in the category, and the HR suite means a company can run hiring and HR through the same vendor as it grows.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby bundles scheduling, CRM, and analytics into one contract without add-ons. Workable's AI layer (Workable Agent), video interviews, and assessments are add-ons on Standard — the plan most growing teams start on. Ashby's funnel analytics and conversion tracking are more detailed than Workable's reporting, and the all-in-one model is a cleaner fit for a team that runs structured recruiting across the full funnel.

### Best fit

**Choose Workable if** you want sourcing, recruiting, and an HR suite from one vendor, and your company's size is steady enough that headcount-based pricing works in your favor. For a company still growing fast, that pricing model deserves a close look.

### Why consider it

Your careers page is a meaningful part of how you attract candidates, and your people team wants to control it without engineering. Teamtailor's careers site builder is designed for non-technical users, with templates, sections, team pages, and media. The ATS sits behind it, tracking candidates through the pipeline and triggering stage automations as they move.

### Key features

* **Drag-and-drop careers site builder.** Teams build and edit a fully branded careers site without coding, using sections, templates, team pages, images, and video.
* **Candidate pipeline with automations.** A visual pipeline with stage automations triggers messages and actions as candidates move through the hiring process.
* **DEI tools.** Anonymous hiring and EEO surveys are built into the platform for teams running structured diversity initiatives.
* **Chrome sourcing extension.** Recruiters add candidates from LinkedIn and other sites directly into the pipeline from a browser extension.
* **AI Co-pilot.** Available on higher tiers, Teamtailor's AI Co-pilot assists with job descriptions, candidate communications, and analysis.

### Pricing

Teamtailor doesn't publish pricing. Sources conflict on the model — some report pricing by job slots, others by company size — and no self-serve rate is available on the site. [Third-party procurement data](https://www.pin.com/blog/teamtailor-pricing/) puts Starter at roughly $300 to $400 per month and Growth at $600 to $1,200, with mid-market contracts running $6,000 to $12,000 per year. No confirmed self-serve free trial is available; demos are offered.

### Where it beats Ashby

Teamtailor's careers site builder gives a people team direct control over the candidate-facing experience without a developer. The depth of careers site customization — drag-and-drop sections, team pages, embedded video, DEI-specific features — is designed for companies where the careers page is part of the brand. That focus on employer branding is not central to what Ashby is built around.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby's analytics, scheduling, and CRM are more comprehensive than Teamtailor's pipeline. Ashby publishes its Foundations pricing with a band-by-band calculator; Teamtailor requires a demo to get any pricing information at all. Ashby's structured hiring tooling goes deeper for a recruiting team that needs funnel conversion tracking and outbound sourcing from the same platform.

### Best fit

**Choose Teamtailor if** your careers site is a core part of your recruiting strategy, your people team wants to manage it without engineering help, and you're comfortable with a quote-based engagement. If your priority is analytics, CRM depth, or transparent pricing, another tool fits better.

## 6. Rippling: Best for companies that want hiring inside their HR and payroll platform

[Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/) is a workforce management platform. HR, payroll, and IT management are the core product; recruiting is an add-on module that sits inside the same system.

### Why consider it

You already use Rippling for HR and payroll, or you're buying the whole Rippling platform, and you want recruiting inside the same contract. When a hire is made in the recruiting module, that record flows into HR, payroll, and IT onboarding without a separate import or integration to maintain. The value is consolidation: the operational handoff from candidate to employee happens in the same platform.

### Key features

* **Unified people platform.** Recruiting lives in the same system as HR, payroll, and IT, so a new hire's record moves from the pipeline to onboarding without re-entry at any step.
* **Job postings and pipeline.** Requisitions are created from the HR system, jobs post to job boards, and applications come back into a candidate pipeline with stage tracking.
* **Interview scheduling.** Scheduling integrates with calendar tools, so hiring managers and candidates can book without a back-and-forth over availability.
* **Offer letters.** Offers are generated from templates inside the platform, with the candidate's details pulled from their record, and routed for e-signature.
* **Native HRIS integration.** Compensation, department, and start date data flow from the offer directly into the employee record — no manual entry at the HR handoff.

### Pricing

Rippling's Recruiting module is an add-on and cannot be purchased on its own. No pricing is published for it. [Augtal](https://augtal.com/blog/rippling-ats-review-2026/) reports the add-on at roughly $150 to $600 per month depending on company size. [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/rippling-pricing/), citing Vendr buyer data, puts the Recruiting module at approximately $4 per user per month, with a median Rippling contract across all modules at $45,360 per year. The core Unity Platform starts at $8 per employee per month. No self-serve free trial is available for the Recruiting module.

### Where it beats Ashby

Rippling's recruiting module sits inside the same platform as payroll and IT, so the operational handoff from candidate to employee requires no integration to build or maintain. Onboarding — accounts, devices, payroll — starts the moment an offer is accepted, all within the same system the company already uses for HR. Ashby is a standalone recruiting platform with no native HR, payroll, or IT management.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby is a purpose-built recruiting platform with scheduling, CRM, and analytics on every plan. Rippling's recruiting module is one part of a larger workforce system and is not sold or supported as a standalone ATS. The recruiting features are less deep than Ashby's, particularly in analytics, structured hiring, and outbound sourcing. Rippling also requires the full platform purchase; Ashby can be evaluated and bought on its own terms.

### Best fit

**Choose Rippling if** you're already on the Rippling platform and want your recruiting and HR consolidated in one contract. If you're buying an ATS on its own merits, a dedicated recruiting platform gives you more depth for the same money.

## 7. BambooHR: Best for hiring inside an HR system of record

[BambooHR](https://www.bamboohr.com/) is an HR platform. Employee records, onboarding, PTO, e-signatures, and reporting are the core product, and the hiring module is one part of it.

### Why consider it

You need a human resources system of record, and you'd like the ATS to come with it. When a candidate is hired, their record becomes their employee record — onboarding tasks, document signing, and time-off accrual pick up from there without an import step. The hiring module exists so the HR team doesn't have to re-enter a new hire.

### Key features

* **HR system of record.** BambooHR keeps employee records, org charts, e-signatures, PTO, reporting, and a mobile app, with payroll, benefits administration, and time tracking as separately priced add-ons.
* **Bamboo AI.** A natural-language layer over the HR data answers workforce questions, flags unusual time-off patterns, and predicts burnout. It does not review job applications.
* **Reports.** Candidate source and funnel reports share the same report builder as headcount, turnover, and time-off data.
* **Background checks.** Orders are placed from inside the candidate's record and results come back through BambooHR's own checking partner.
* **Offer letter templates.** On higher tiers, BambooHR generates offer letters from templates with candidate details filled in, sends them for e-signature, and files them to the employee record on acceptance.

### Pricing

BambooHR [publishes per-employee rates](https://www.bamboohr.com/pricing). Core is $10, Pro is $17, and Elite is $25 per employee per month, with a flat minimum of $250 per month for companies of 25 or fewer employees. Applicant tracking is included from Core, with job opening caps of 5 on Core, 25 on Pro, and 50 on Elite. Payroll, benefits administration, and time tracking are separately priced. A 40-person company on Core pays $400 per month for the HR system with the hiring module included.

### Where it beats Ashby

BambooHR has a mature HRIS where the candidate record becomes the employee record at hire, with no integration to maintain. Employee records, PTO, and e-signatures have been in the product for years, and onboarding tasks begin the moment an offer is accepted. Ashby is a recruiting platform only, so HR, payroll, and onboarding are separate purchases from separate vendors.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby is a recruiting platform with scheduling, CRM, and funnel analytics. BambooHR's hiring module has no sourcing database, no outbound CRM, and no interview scheduling, and job openings are capped at 5, 25, or 50 by HR tier. Ashby is built for a team focused on hiring; BambooHR's hiring module serves a team focused on managing the people already hired.

### Best fit

**Choose BambooHR if** you need a human resources system of record and want the ATS to come with it. If you're buying an ATS as the primary tool, Ashby or Polymer is a better fit than a module inside an HR platform.

## How to choose an Ashby alternative

Work through these before you book any demos. Most teams can rule out half the list in ten minutes.

* **Hiring volume.** How many roles are open at once? Polymer's plans size by published jobs (5, 20, or 50). BambooHR caps jobs by HR tier. Ashby and Greenhouse are built for many parallel roles managed by a recruiting team.
* **Recruiting team structure.** Do you have dedicated recruiters, or do hiring managers post and review for their own openings? Ashby and Greenhouse assume a talent acquisition team. Lever assumes recruiters running outbound work. Polymer assumes the hiring manager is doing the hiring.
* **Need for sourcing or a recruiting CRM.** If outbound sourcing is part of a recruiter's daily work, Lever or Workable has the tooling for it. If you hire from inbound applications, that layer goes unused in both.
* **Platform consolidation.** Do you want your ATS inside a larger platform — HR, payroll, or workforce management? BambooHR and Rippling give you that. Ashby and Polymer are standalone recruiting platforms.
* **Implementation tolerance.** Greenhouse is a multi-week project. Ashby is designed for a recruiting team to configure over days. Polymer is running the day you sign up.
* **AI requirements.** Decide whether you want AI screening included, as an add-on, or not at all. Polymer includes Plato on every plan with nothing to configure. Ashby includes AI credits on every plan; the AI Notetaker is a paid add-on. Workable sells its AI separately. BambooHR has no application screening AI.
* **Budget and pricing model.** Ask how the price changes as the company grows. Headcount-based pricing (Ashby, Greenhouse, Workable, Lever, Rippling) rises with the team. Job-based pricing (Polymer) rises with hiring volume. Per-employee pricing (BambooHR) rises with every hire, including the ones not involved in hiring at all.

## Which Ashby alternative is right for you?

How much recruiting software does your company actually need? Every product on this list takes a different view on what hiring software should do — and for whom. ### Do I need an enterprise ATS like Ashby?

Probably not, unless your company has a recruiting team running structured hiring across many roles in parallel. ### How does Ashby compare to Greenhouse?

Ashby and Greenhouse are both built for dedicated recruiting teams, but they take different approaches. Greenhouse is priced by quote only, requires a multi-week implementation, and is built for large talent acquisition departments running structured, competency-based hiring with approval chains and a 500+ integration marketplace. Teams comparing the two are usually asking whether they need Greenhouse's depth of structured hiring tooling and ecosystem breadth, or whether Ashby's all-in-one model covers more ground in a single contract.
