## Is Ashby the right fit for your team?

### Where Ashby is strongest

Ashby is a widely used applicant tracking system (ATS), and for some companies it's the right one. Its defining feature is consolidation: the ATS, a sourcing and CRM layer, interview scheduling, and analytics all live in one platform under one contract. A recruiting team that would otherwise pay three vendors gets everything in Ashby. Its analytics are built in across all plans, covering funnel conversion, pipeline health, and source reporting, so a head of talent acquisition can track recruiting metrics without exporting to a spreadsheet. Ashby also includes AI features across the platform — AI-assisted application review, an AI candidate assistant for sourcing outreach — with an AI notetaker available as a paid add-on. The scheduling tooling handles complex multi-panel interviews without a separate calendar tool.

**Choose Ashby if** you have a dedicated recruiting team that will use the CRM, sourcing, and analytics every day, and your company's headcount is stable enough that headcount-based pricing works in your favour.

### How Ashby is priced

Ashby prices its All-In-One plans by total company headcount. The Foundations plan, for companies up to 100 employees, publishes a price calculator with five bands: $300 per month for 1–10 employees, $400 for 11–25, $500 for 26–50, $700 for 51–75, and $900 for 76–100. A 10% discount applies on annual commitments. All-In-One Plus, for companies of 101–1,000 employees, and All-In-One Enterprise, for 1,000 or more, are quote only. Third-party sources report a median buyer paying around $20,939 per year across all tiers. Ashby also sells a standalone Analytics product for companies with 100 or more employees that want Ashby's reporting on top of a different ATS; that is priced by quote as well.

### What's included and what's added on

Every All-In-One plan includes the ATS, sourcing and CRM, scheduling, and analytics, plus AI credits at no additional cost. Additional credits run $500 per year for 5,000. The AI notetaker — which transcribes and structures interview notes — is a paid add-on on every plan. Ashby's pricing page does not publish the add-on price for it.

### Who tends to look elsewhere

Companies look for alternatives for a few reasons. The platform is built around the assumption that a dedicated recruiting team will use all of it: the CRM, the sourcing tools, the scheduling layer, and the analytics dashboards. For a company where the hiring manager and a founder do the hiring themselves, most of that goes untouched. The headcount-based pricing model also means the bill rises as the company grows, independent of whether hiring volume changes. And while Ashby's Foundations tier does publish prices, the platform's setup and feature depth can be more than a growing team wants to onboard. If a company wants something they can get up and running in a day and use across a small, collaborative team, the complexity of Ashby's full platform can feel like overhead.

If one of those sounds like you, the seven tools below cover the range of alternatives, from an easy-to-adopt ATS for growing companies to enterprise suites for structured, high-volume hiring.

## 1. Polymer: Best for growing companies

[Polymer](https://www.polymer.co/) is a modern, intuitive ATS for growing companies, from a founder interviewing their first hires to a company with hiring managers and a recruiter. Users are unlimited, so anyone you want involved in hiring gets a seat. Plans are tiered by how many jobs you need open at once, not by how many people you employ.

### Why consider it

You want a complete, modern applicant tracking system you can start using today, with a beautiful branded careers page, easy team collaboration, and built-in AI candidate screening and analysis. Polymer covers the full hiring workflow — custom application forms, automations, job distribution, and an API — but skips the administrative weight of enterprise recruiting software. Setting up is an afternoon's work, not weeks of onboarding.

Polymer is intentionally focused, and that's what makes it easy to use. It has enough capability to support a professional, structured hiring process and none of the workflows and complexity your team doesn't need. A growing company can have sophisticated hiring requirements while still valuing speed, usability, and flexibility.

### Key features

* **Message automations and templates.** Messaging automations save the time and effort of writing the same candidate emails. Automatically trigger messages to candidates upon hiring stage moves and make your process repeatable across jobs by creating message templates.
* **Plato AI screening.** Instead of reviewing every candidate as they come in, have Plato AI analyze your candidates and filter by fit score so you review your strongest applicants first. Plato provides an evidence-based analysis, which lets you verify its reasoning. Quickly move poor and weak candidates to the archive section via bulk move.
* **Candidate reviews and review requests.** Polymer gives you an easy way to get colleagues to leave candidate reviews, whether they're on the hiring team or not. You can grant access to a single candidate's review kit via an email notification.
* **Company job board.** A branded careers page makes your company look professional and polished to candidates. Add your branding to your board, put it on your own domain, and select from different languages. You can embed the board on your site, sync it into Webflow, or build your own site on the API. * **Customizations.** Configure Polymer to fit your workflow in multiple ways, including a customer API that lets you manage candidates, new application notifications sent to Slack or Discord, your own Google Analytics stream ID, automatic posting to your LinkedIn company page, Zapier triggers for hiring events, and a completely custom hiring pipeline.

### Pricing

Polymer publishes its [pricing](https://www.polymer.co/pricing). Starter is $124 per month, Growth is $233, and Scale is $415, each billed annually. Month to month, they're $149, $279, and $499. Every plan has the complete feature set, unlimited users, and unlimited candidates. The plans differ by how many jobs you can have published at once (5, 20, or 50) and by monthly Plato reviews (50, 100, or 150).

You can create jobs, build the board, and add your team before you enter a payment method. A 14-day free trial starts when you publish your first job, and you can switch plans during the trial without ending it.

### Where it beats Ashby

Polymer is designed for the team where a hiring manager and a founder do the hiring themselves, and that ease of adoption is where it's ahead of Ashby. There's no CRM to configure, no analytics dashboard to initialize, and no scheduling layer to wire up before a job goes live. A job needs only a title and a description to publish. Plato AI's candidate screening needs nothing more than that — turn it on and it reviews applicants against the job description you already wrote, with no ideal candidate profile to build first. The job board, the hiring stages, and the message templates are ready to go out of the box and can be customized whenever you like. Polymer is easy to adopt without sacrificing the flexibility to run the hiring process your own way.

Ashby's Foundations pricing rises by headcount band as a company grows, whether or not hiring volume changes. Polymer's plans are tiered by how many jobs a company needs open at once, so a company whose headcount is growing but whose hiring needs remain unchanged pays the same as before.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby bundles a sourcing and CRM layer, multi-panel scheduling, and built-in analytics across all plans — tools Polymer doesn't have. For a recruiting team that sources passive candidates, tracks long-term nurture, and runs weekly pipeline metrics, that consolidation is Ashby's main value.

### Best fit

**Choose Polymer if** you're a growing company that wants a capable, flexible ATS that's ready to use the day you sign up. If you need a sourcing CRM, interview scheduling at scale, or deep recruiting analytics across the team, Ashby is the better choice.

## 2. Greenhouse: Best for enterprise recruiting teams

[Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/) is built for a talent acquisition department. If you have recruiters, recruiting coordinators, and a head of TA who reports hiring metrics to leadership, this is the system that department would pick.

### Why consider it

You need a comprehensive suite of enterprise recruiting tools, and you have a dedicated team to manage it. Before a role opens, your team defines the competencies for it, writes an interview plan, assigns each interviewer a kit of questions, and sets the rubric they'll score against. Interviewers submit scorecards before the debrief so the panel discusses the scores, not anchors on whoever spoke first.

### Key features

* **Interview plans and kits.** Each stage of each role gets a plan covering interviewers, competencies, questions, and a scorecard rubric with focus attributes; AI drafts role-specific interview questions and scorecard attributes to start from.
* **Requisition and offer approvals.** Roles open after an approval chain signs off, and offers go through their own approval chain before a candidate sees one; an admin sets up both chains and chooses the approvers.
* **Reporting.** Greenhouse reports on pipeline, source, time-to-fill, offer acceptance, and demographics, with a report builder for custom queries and AI-built report filters on higher tiers.
* **Integrations marketplace.** Deep integrations, an open API, and Model Context Protocol support connect Greenhouse to hundreds of assessment, background check, scheduling, and HRIS partners.
* **Generative AI.** Greenhouse AI surfaces candidates, drafts scorecard attributes, summarizes interviewer feedback, and flags candidate fraud through Real Talent™; resume anonymization is available on higher tiers.

### Pricing

Greenhouse doesn't publish pricing. Plans are quote only, across Core, Plus, and Pro, priced by company headcount. Third-party sources report Core at roughly $5,100 to $12,000 per year for small companies, Plus at $15,000 to $35,000, and Pro at $40,000 or more. A sales cycle and an annual contract are standard. Implementation typically takes several weeks for mid-market teams.

### Where it beats Ashby

Greenhouse has a structured hiring framework that Ashby doesn't match at the same depth: interview kits with assigned competencies, scorecards submitted before the debrief, and a report builder that tracks where each stage slows and which sources produce hires. Its integration marketplace — with hundreds of assessment, background check, and HRIS partners, plus Model Context Protocol support for external AI tools — is also the widest in the category, so a tool your team already uses is more likely to have a native connection.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby bundles sourcing, CRM, and scheduling in one contract, while Greenhouse is recruiting software only; sourcing and scheduling are separate purchases. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier, while Greenhouse requires a sales call before any pricing is shared. Ashby's analytics are included on every plan without a separate report-builder add-on, and its AI features work from published job descriptions without additional setup. Greenhouse's scorecard-heavy process also requires significant admin work to configure per role, which adds time before the first job goes live.

### Best fit

**Choose Greenhouse if** you have a recruiting team that will run structured hiring, someone to administer the system, and the budget for a multi-week implementation. If hiring managers run their own searches without a recruiting coordinator, Greenhouse is built for a larger operation.

## 3. Lever: Best for teams that source passive candidates

[Lever](https://www.lever.co/) is an ATS and a recruiting CRM in one database. It's built for a recruiting team that spends its days sourcing people who haven't applied and staying in touch until a role opens.

### Why consider it

You want a dedicated recruiting CRM alongside an ATS. Recruiters source passive candidates, tag them, put them in nurture campaigns, and log every touchpoint over months or years. When the right role opens, the record becomes an application. If you have recruiters whose job is building that pipeline, Lever is organized around it.

### Key features

* **Long-term nurture campaigns.** Multi-step email sequences keep a passive candidate warm for months or years before a role opens, with tracking on opens and replies and rules for when someone drops out of the sequence.
* **Loss-risk alerts.** Every active candidate gets a score that flags fading engagement based on response times and activity, so a recruiter can step in before an offer is declined.
* **Talent Fit scoring.** AI scores candidates against role criteria, surfacing the strongest applicants across the pipeline for a recruiter's review.
* **LinkedIn Recruiter System Connect.** Lever pushes jobs to LinkedIn, and a two-way sync with LinkedIn Recruiter puts InMail activity and profile data on the Lever record for teams that pay for Recruiter.
* **Generative AI.** Lever's AI features include interview transcripts and summaries, Fraud Signals for candidate verification, and an optional AI Screening add-on built on VONQ that scores applicants against the role before a recruiter reviews them.

### Pricing

Lever's pricing is a custom quote. The pricing page shows no published numbers. Third-party sources report roughly $6,700 to $15,400 per year for small companies, with a median negotiated contract around $15,399 across all sizes. Annual contracts are standard. Lever is owned by Employ, the same parent as JazzHR and Jobvite.

### Where it beats Ashby

Lever's NurtureKit puts passive candidate outreach at the center of the platform in a way Ashby's CRM layer doesn't match. Recruiters build multi-step sequences, track who opens and replies, set exit rules, and keep a candidate warm across months before a role opens. Every touchpoint logs on the candidate's record, so the conversation history is complete when the right role finally does open.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby bundles scheduling and analytics with its ATS and CRM, while Lever has no native interview scheduling and no built-in analytics suite comparable to Ashby's. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier; Lever publishes no pricing at all. Ashby's AI features are included on every plan; Lever's AI Screening is an optional paid add-on. Lever also has no HR suite, no assessments, and no native video interviews, so teams that need those capabilities need separate tools.

### Best fit

**Choose Lever if** you have recruiters who will use the CRM and run nurture campaigns daily. If your candidates come from applications to your job posts, most of Lever goes unused.

## 4. Workable: Best for companies that want sourcing and HR from one vendor

[Workable](https://www.workable.com/) is an ATS with a sourcing database, an HR suite, and published pricing. It's built for a company that wants recruiting and HR administration from one platform and is large enough that headcount-based pricing is predictable.

### Why consider it

You want sourcing, recruiting, and HR administration from one vendor. Workable's sourcing database has more than 400 million profiles, and AI sourcing is built on top. Its HR suite covers employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation, so one vendor handles both sides of the hire.

### Key features

* **Sourcing database.** Over 400 million candidate profiles with AI sourcing built in, letting a recruiting team run outbound search from inside the ATS without a separate tool.
* **HR suite.** Employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation sit alongside the ATS in one platform, so recruiting and HR share the same system.
* **Video interviews and assessments.** Native video interviews and candidate assessments are included on Premier and Enterprise; on Standard they are separately priced add-ons.
* **Texting.** Two-way candidate texting is built into the platform; on Standard it is a paid add-on and included from Premier onward.
* **Generative AI.** Workable Agent is a paid add-on on every plan that evaluates candidates against an ideal profile, sources passive candidates, and handles candidate chat; it runs on credits starting at $600 for 5,000.

### Pricing

Workable publishes pricing by total company headcount. Standard is $299 per month billed annually for 1–20 employees, Premier is $599, and Enterprise is $719. Standard is also available month to month at $360 per month. At 21–50 employees those prices rise to $419, $829, and $989. Workable HR is a separate add-on at $99 per month on annual billing for 1–20 employees.

### Where it beats Ashby

Workable has a mature sourcing database with more than 400 million profiles and an HR suite that covers what happens after the hire — things Ashby doesn't offer. For a company that wants recruiting and HR in one contract, Workable covers both sides where Ashby covers only recruiting.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby's analytics are built in across all plans and cover funnel, pipeline, and source reporting without additional configuration. Workable's AI features are a paid add-on on every plan and require building an ideal candidate profile per job before reviewing applicants; Ashby's AI is included in its AI credits and works from the job description. Ashby also includes scheduling natively, while Workable relies on integrations for interview scheduling. Workable's Standard plan lacks video interviews and assessments without additional purchases.

### Best fit

**Choose Workable if** your company wants sourcing, recruiting, and HR administration from one vendor and your headcount is steady enough that headcount-based pricing works in your favour. ## 5. Rippling: Best for hiring inside an HR and payroll platform

[Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/) is a workforce management platform. HR, payroll, benefits, device management, and app provisioning are the product, and the recruiting module is one part of it — available only to companies already on the Rippling platform.

### Why consider it

You are already on Rippling for HR and payroll, or you want to run the full employee lifecycle — from job post through payroll — in one system. The candidate record converts to an employee record at hire, and the new hire's payroll, benefits, and device provisioning flow from the same system without re-entry.

### Key features

* **Unified workforce platform.** HR, payroll, benefits, device management, and recruiting share one employee record, so a hire moves from offer to onboarded without data being re-entered across systems.
* **Requisition and offer management.** Hiring managers submit requisitions, and offers are generated and approved inside the platform, with the final offer letter flowing straight into the employee record.
* **Interview scheduling.** Scheduling is built into the recruiting module, coordinating interviewer availability and sending candidates calendar invites without a separate tool.
* **Role-based permissions.** Access is set per user and per module, so a hiring manager sees only their own roles and candidates, and an HR admin sees everything.
* **App provisioning on hire.** When a candidate is hired, Rippling can automatically provision the accounts, apps, and devices the role requires, based on the department and role configuration.

### Pricing

Rippling doesn't publish pricing for the recruiting module. The core Unity Platform starts at a reported $8 per employee per month. The recruiting module is an add-on, quoted separately; independent sources report it at roughly $4 per user per month, with total recruiting costs estimated at $150 to $600 per month depending on company size. A sales call is required for any pricing details. There is no self-serve trial for the recruiting module.

### Where it beats Ashby

Rippling connects recruiting to payroll, benefits, and device provisioning in a way Ashby doesn't. When a candidate is hired, the downstream HR and IT setup starts automatically from the same platform. For a company that runs its entire workforce on Rippling, adding the recruiting module avoids a separate ATS integration entirely.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby is a purpose-built recruiting platform with a deep sourcing CRM, scheduling, and analytics that the Rippling recruiting module doesn't match. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier and can be evaluated independently; Rippling's recruiting module requires a sales call and cannot be purchased as a standalone product. Rippling's recruiting module is primarily designed for the HR team, not for a team where hiring managers run their own searches.

### Best fit

**Choose Rippling if** your company already runs HR, payroll, and benefits on Rippling and wants recruiting inside the same system. If your team wants a standalone ATS it can evaluate and start using today, Rippling isn't designed for that.

## 6. Teamtailor: Best for employer branding-first recruiting

[Teamtailor](https://www.teamtailor.com/) is an ATS built around the careers site. Employer branding, candidate experience, and DEI tooling are where it focuses, and the applicant tracking sits inside that.

### Why consider it

Your company's careers site is the primary way candidates find you, and you want the ATS vendor to own the whole experience. Teamtailor's careers site builder is drag-and-drop, with no coding required, and it supports video content, employee testimonials, and culture pages alongside the job listings.

### Key features

* **Careers site builder.** A drag-and-drop editor builds careers pages with video, employee stories, culture sections, and job listings without a developer or a separate CMS; teams control the full candidate experience from one place.
* **Anonymous hiring.** Candidate names and photos are hidden from the pipeline until a recruiter chooses to reveal them, reducing early-stage bias in the review process.
* **DEI tools.** EEO surveys, anonymous application review, and diversity reporting are built into the platform for teams that track representation across the funnel.
* **Chrome sourcing extension.** Recruiters add profiles from LinkedIn and other sites directly into the candidate database from a browser extension, without leaving the page they're on.
* **Generative AI.** AI Co-pilot, available on higher tiers, drafts job descriptions, generates interview questions, and summarizes candidate profiles; a candidate chatbot on the careers site handles applicant questions automatically.

### Pricing

Teamtailor doesn't publish pricing. Plans are quote only. Third-party sources report Starter at roughly $300 to $400 per month, Growth at $600 to $1,200, and Enterprise at $1,500 or more, with mid-market annual contracts commonly in the range of $6,000 to $12,000.

### Where it beats Ashby

Teamtailor's careers site builder is the product's center of gravity in a way Ashby's job board is not. Teams build multi-page careers sites with culture content, video, and employee stories without touching code. Anonymous hiring and EEO reporting are built in rather than bolted on, which matters for teams that make DEI a formal part of the screening process.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby's analytics are built in across all plans, while Teamtailor's reporting is lighter on lower tiers. Ashby's scheduling, CRM, and sourcing are part of the core platform; Teamtailor's sourcing is limited to a Chrome extension with no native CRM layer. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier; Teamtailor requires a sales conversation for any pricing information. Teamtailor's AI Co-pilot is gated to higher tiers, so smaller plans get no AI assistance in the pipeline.

### Best fit

**Choose Teamtailor if** your careers site is the primary channel for attracting candidates and your team wants to own that experience end to end without a developer. If deep recruiting analytics and CRM matter more than branding, Ashby is the stronger fit.

## 7. Pinpoint: Best for mid-market in-house recruiting teams

[Pinpoint](https://www.pinpointhq.com/) is an in-house recruiting platform for companies running several hiring streams at once — desk-based and frontline, early-career and executive, agency-sourced and direct — all in one system.

### Why consider it

You're a mid-market company managing several different hiring streams, working with recruiting agencies, and running employee referrals alongside direct applications. Pinpoint covers all three, with an agency portal, a referral program, an onboarding module, and approval chains keyed to salary band.

### Key features

* **Lifecycle automation.** If-this-then-that rules across the candidate lifecycle auto-advance on an assessment score, send templated communications at each step, and route an offer through the approval chain for its salary band.
* **Agency portal.** External recruiters get their own login to submit candidates into the pipeline, see where each one stands, and track fees, without needing an internal seat.
* **Onboarding module.** Hires move from the offer stage into an onboarding module with task lists, document collection, and start-date reminders for the new hire and the manager.
* **Background and reference checks.** A recruiter orders both from the candidate's record and tracks them to completion inside the platform, with results attached to the candidate.
* **Generative AI.** Available on the Enterprise tier, Pinpoint includes a hiring copilot that answers plain-language questions and drafts communications, a notetaker that transcribes interviews, a candidate chatbot on the careers site, and candidate match scores against criteria a recruiter sets.

### Pricing

Pinpoint doesn't publish a rate card. Contracts are annual and there's no free trial. Third-party reviewers report Growth at roughly $345 per month on annual billing and Enterprise at $1,200 or more, with annual costs estimated between $4,600 and $15,000. AI features, blind screening, and API access are all on Enterprise only.

### Where it beats Ashby

Pinpoint has an agency portal and an employee referral program that Ashby doesn't. External recruiters submit candidates through their own login, and employees refer through a tracked program. Lifecycle automation moves candidates on assessment scores and routes each offer for approval by salary band. Background checks, reference checks, and video interviews are included in the platform without a separate purchase.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby has a sourcing database and CRM built for outbound recruiting, while Pinpoint tracks the applicants who come to you and has no outbound sourcing layer. Ashby's analytics are built in across all plans; Pinpoint gates AI and advanced analytics to Enterprise. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier; Pinpoint requires a sales conversation for any pricing information. Growth-tier Pinpoint teams get no AI assistance in the hiring pipeline at all.

### Best fit

**Choose Pinpoint if** you're a mid-market in-house recruiting team managing multiple hiring streams, working with agencies, and prepared to sign an annual contract. If your team is a few people hiring for a handful of roles at a time, it's built for a bigger operation than yours.

## How to choose an Ashby alternative

Work through these before you book any demos. Most teams can rule out half the list in ten minutes.

* **Recruiting team structure.** Do you have dedicated recruiters, or do hiring managers do the hiring themselves? Ashby, Greenhouse, and Lever assume a talent acquisition operation. Polymer assumes the hiring manager is doing the hiring alongside a founder or a small team.
* **Need for sourcing or a recruiting CRM.** If outbound sourcing is part of a recruiter's daily work, Lever, Ashby, or Workable has the tooling for it. If you hire from inbound applications, most of that tooling goes unused.
* **Hiring volume and concurrent roles.** How many jobs are open at once, and how many applications does each one get? Polymer's plans are sized by published jobs (5, 20, or 50). Ashby's plans are sized by headcount. Pinpoint and Teamtailor both require annual contracts sized for a mid-market operation.
* **Workflow complexity and flexibility.** Do you need approval chains by salary band, multi-stream pipelines, and an agency portal? Or custom hiring stages and application forms per job? Pinpoint is the former. Polymer is the latter. For [talent acquisition teams](/blog/talent-acquisition-vs-recruitment) running structured processes, Greenhouse or Ashby fits better.
* **Implementation tolerance.** Greenhouse is a multi-week implementation. Ashby's Foundations tier is self-serve but has a full platform to configure. Polymer is running the day you sign up.
* **AI requirements.** Decide whether you want AI screening included, as an add-on, or not at all. Polymer includes it on every plan with nothing to configure. Ashby includes AI credits on every plan. Workable sells AI separately. Pinpoint gates it to Enterprise. Rippling has no recruiting-focused AI.
* **Integration and HR needs.** If you want recruiting inside your HR and payroll system, Rippling is worth evaluating. If you need a wide integration marketplace, Greenhouse has the most partners. Polymer's API manages candidates and job data on every plan.
* **Budget and pricing model.** Ask how the price moves as your company grows. Headcount-based pricing (Ashby, Greenhouse, Workable) rises with the company. Job-based pricing (Polymer) rises with hiring volume. Flat annual contracts (Pinpoint) are predictable but require a commitment. Published pricing (Polymer, Workable) lets you evaluate without a sales call.

## Which Ashby alternative is right for you?

How much recruiting software does your company actually need? Every product on this list differs in which features it focuses on, how complex those features are, and how much process is built in around them. What your team needs from those three is what decides which one fits. Some of that built-in process is overkill for a smaller team and causes confusion and slow adoption. Some of it is necessary structure for a larger recruiting operation.
