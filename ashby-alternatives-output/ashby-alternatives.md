## Is Ashby the right fit for your team?

### Where Ashby is strongest

Ashby is a widely used applicant tracking system (ATS), and for some companies it's the right one. It consolidates the ATS, a sourcing and CRM layer, interview scheduling, and analytics into one platform under one contract. A recruiting team that would otherwise pay three separate vendor contracts gets everything in Ashby. Its analytics are built in across all plans, covering funnel conversion, pipeline health, and source reporting, so a head of talent acquisition can track recruiting metrics without exporting to a spreadsheet. The platform includes AI-assisted application review and an AI candidate assistant for sourcing outreach, and a separate AI notetaker is available as a paid add-on. The scheduling tooling handles complex multi-panel interviews without a separate calendar tool.

**Choose Ashby if** you have a dedicated recruiting team that will use the CRM, sourcing, and analytics every day, and your company's headcount is stable enough that headcount-based pricing works in your favour.

### How Ashby is priced

Ashby prices its All-In-One plans by total company headcount. The Foundations plan, for companies up to 100 employees, publishes a price calculator with five bands — $300 per month for 1–10 employees, $400 for 11–25, $500 for 26–50, $700 for 51–75, and $900 for 76–100. A 10% discount applies on annual commitments. All-In-One Plus, for companies of 101–1,000 employees, and All-In-One Enterprise, for 1,000 or more, are quote only. Third-party sources report, across all tiers, a median buyer paying around $20,939 per year. Ashby also sells a standalone Analytics product for companies with 100 or more employees that want Ashby's reporting on top of a different ATS; that is priced by quote as well.

### What's included and what's added on

Every All-In-One plan includes the ATS, sourcing and CRM, scheduling, and analytics, plus AI credits at no additional cost. Additional credits run $500 per year for 5,000. The AI notetaker, which transcribes and structures interview notes, is a paid add-on on every plan. Ashby does not publish the notetaker's price.

### Who tends to look elsewhere

Teams leave Ashby for a few consistent reasons. The platform is built around the assumption that a dedicated recruiting team will use all of it, including the CRM, the sourcing tools, the scheduling layer, and the analytics dashboards. At a company where the hiring manager and a founder do the hiring themselves, most of those features go untouched. The headcount-based pricing model also means the bill rises as the company grows, independent of whether hiring volume changes. Ashby's Foundations tier publishes prices, but the platform's setup and feature depth can exceed what a growing team wants to take on. A company that wants something up and running in a day, used across a small collaborative team, will find Ashby's full platform too broad.

If either of those describes your situation, the seven tools below range from an easy-to-adopt ATS for growing companies to enterprise suites for structured, high-volume hiring.

## 1. Polymer: Best for growing companies

[Polymer](https://www.polymer.co/) is a modern, intuitive ATS for growing companies, from a founder interviewing their first hires to a company with hiring managers and a recruiter. Every seat is included at no extra cost — invite anyone you want involved in hiring. Polymer's plans are tiered by how many jobs a company needs open at once, not by how many people it employs.

### Why consider it

You want a complete, modern applicant tracking system you can start using today, with a beautiful branded careers page, easy team collaboration, and built-in AI candidate screening and analysis. Polymer covers the full hiring workflow, including custom application forms, automations, job distribution, and an API, but skips the administrative weight of enterprise recruiting software. Setting up takes an afternoon, not weeks of onboarding.

Polymer is focused enough to stay easy to use and capable enough to support a professional, structured hiring process — no extraneous workflows, no unnecessary complexity. Growing companies need sophisticated hiring capabilities and fast, flexible tools they can actually use.

### Key features

* **Message automations and templates.** Messaging automations eliminate repeated candidate emails. Messages trigger automatically when a candidate moves to a new hiring stage, and message templates make the process repeatable across jobs.
* **Plato AI screening.** Instead of reviewing every candidate as they come in, have Plato AI analyze your candidates and filter by fit score so you review your strongest applicants first. Plato provides an evidence-based analysis, which lets you verify its reasoning. Quickly move poor and weak candidates to the archive section via bulk move.
* **Candidate reviews and review requests.** Colleagues can leave candidate reviews through a review kit sent by email notification, whether they're on the hiring team or not.
* **Company job board.** A branded careers page makes your company look professional and polished to candidates. Add your branding to your board, put it on your own domain, and select from different languages. You can embed the board on your site, sync it into Webflow, or build your own site on the API. Customizable application forms offer multiple question formats, including file uploads.
* **Customizations.** Configure Polymer to fit your workflow in multiple ways, including a public API for managing candidates, Slack or Discord notifications for new applications, a custom Google Analytics stream ID, automatic posting to your LinkedIn company page, Zapier triggers for hiring events, and a fully custom hiring pipeline.

### Pricing

Polymer publishes its [pricing](https://www.polymer.co/pricing). The Starter plan is $124 per month, Growth is $233, and Scale is $415, all billed annually. Month to month, they're $149, $279, and $499. Every plan has the complete feature set, unlimited users, and unlimited candidates. The plans differ by how many jobs you can have published at once (5, 20, or 50) and by monthly Plato reviews (50, 100, or 150).

You can create jobs, build the board, and add your team before you enter a payment method. A 14-day free trial starts when you publish your first job, and you can switch plans during the trial without ending it.

### Where it beats Ashby

Polymer is built for the team where a hiring manager and a founder do the hiring themselves, and it is faster and simpler to adopt than Ashby. Ashby requires configuring a CRM, initializing an analytics dashboard, and wiring up a scheduling layer before a job goes live. In Polymer, a job needs only a title and a description to publish. Plato AI's candidate screening requires nothing beyond that. Turn it on and it reviews applicants against the job description you already wrote, with no ideal candidate profile to build first. The job board, the hiring stages, and the message templates are ready to go out of the box and can be customized whenever you like. Adoption is straightforward, and the flexibility to run the hiring process your own way is built in.

Ashby's Foundations pricing rises by headcount band as a company grows, whether or not hiring volume changes. Polymer's plans are tiered by how many jobs a company needs open at once, so a company whose headcount is growing but whose hiring needs remain unchanged pays the same rate.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby bundles a sourcing and CRM layer, multi-panel scheduling, and built-in analytics across all plans — features Polymer does not include. For a recruiting team that sources passive candidates, tracks long-term nurture, and runs weekly pipeline metrics, that consolidation is Ashby's core advantage.

### Best fit

**Choose Polymer if** you're a growing company that wants a capable, flexible ATS that's ready to use the day you sign up. If you need a sourcing CRM, interview scheduling at scale, or deep recruiting analytics across the team, Ashby is the better choice.

## 2. Greenhouse: Best for enterprise recruiting teams

[Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/) is built for a talent acquisition department. If you have recruiters, recruiting coordinators, and a head of TA who reports hiring metrics to leadership, this is the system that department would pick.

### Why consider it

Your team needs enterprise recruiting tools and has a dedicated person to manage them. Before a role opens, your team defines the competencies for it, writes an interview plan, and assigns each interviewer a kit of questions with a rubric to score against. Interviewers submit scorecards before the debrief, so the panel discusses the scores rather than anchoring on whoever spoke first.

### Key features

* **Interview plans and kits.** Each stage of each role gets a plan covering interviewers, competencies, questions, and a scorecard rubric with focus attributes. AI drafts role-specific interview questions and scorecard attributes to start from.
* **Requisition and offer approvals.** Roles open after an approval chain signs off. Offers go through their own approval chain before a candidate sees one. An admin sets up both chains and chooses the approvers.
* **Reporting.** Greenhouse reports on pipeline, source, time-to-fill, offer acceptance, and demographics, with a report builder for custom queries and AI-built report filters on higher tiers.
* **Integrations marketplace.** Deep integrations, an open API, and Model Context Protocol support connect Greenhouse to hundreds of assessment, background check, scheduling, and HRIS partners.
* **Generative AI.** Greenhouse AI surfaces candidates, drafts scorecard attributes, summarizes interviewer feedback, and flags candidate fraud through Real Talent™; resume anonymization is available on higher tiers.

### Pricing

Greenhouse doesn't publish pricing. Plans are quote only, across Core, Plus, and Pro, priced by company headcount. Third-party sources report Core at roughly $5,100 to $12,000 per year for small companies, Plus at $15,000 to $35,000, and Pro at $40,000 or more. A sales cycle and an annual contract are standard. Mid-market teams typically spend three to six weeks on implementation.

### Where it beats Ashby

Greenhouse has a structured hiring framework that Ashby doesn't match at the same depth, including interview kits with assigned competencies, scorecards submitted before the debrief, and a report builder that tracks where each stage slows and which sources produce hires. Its integration marketplace — with hundreds of assessment, background check, and HRIS partners, plus Model Context Protocol support for external AI tools — is also the widest in the category, making a native connection to a tool your team already uses more likely than with competing platforms.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby bundles sourcing, CRM, and scheduling in one contract. Greenhouse is recruiting software only, meaning sourcing and scheduling are separate purchases. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier, but Greenhouse requires a sales call before sharing any pricing. Ashby's analytics are included on every plan without a separate report-builder add-on, and its AI features work from published job descriptions without additional setup. Greenhouse's scorecard-heavy process also requires significant admin work to configure per role, which adds time before the first job goes live.

### Best fit

**Choose Greenhouse if** you have a recruiting team that will run structured hiring, someone to administer the system, and the budget for a multi-week implementation. Hiring managers who run their own searches without a recruiting coordinator will find Greenhouse overbuilt for their needs.

## 3. Lever: Best for teams that source passive candidates

[Lever](https://www.lever.co/) is an ATS and a recruiting CRM in one database. It's built for a recruiting team that spends its days sourcing people who haven't applied and staying in touch until a role opens.

### Why consider it

You want a dedicated recruiting CRM alongside an ATS. Recruiters source passive candidates, tag them, put them in nurture campaigns, and log every touchpoint over months or years. When the right role opens, the record becomes an application. If you have recruiters whose job is building that pipeline, Lever is organized around it.

### Key features

* **Long-term nurture campaigns.** Multi-step email sequences keep a passive candidate warm for months or years before a role opens, with tracking on opens and replies and rules for when someone drops out of the sequence.
* **Loss-risk alerts.** Every active candidate gets a score that flags fading engagement based on response times and activity, so a recruiter can step in before an offer is declined.
* **Talent Fit scoring.** AI scores candidates against role criteria, surfacing the strongest applicants across the pipeline for a recruiter's review.
* **LinkedIn Recruiter System Connect.** Lever pushes jobs to LinkedIn, and a two-way sync with LinkedIn Recruiter puts InMail activity and profile data on the Lever record for teams that pay for Recruiter.
* **Generative AI.** Lever's AI features include interview transcripts and summaries, Fraud Signals for candidate verification, and an AI Screening add-on that scores applicants against the role before a recruiter reviews them.

### Pricing

Lever's pricing is a custom quote. The pricing page shows no published numbers. Third-party sources report roughly $6,700 to $15,400 per year for small companies and a median negotiated contract of $15,399 across all sizes. Annual contracts are standard. Lever is owned by Employ, the same parent as JazzHR and Jobvite.

### Where it beats Ashby

Lever's NurtureKit puts passive candidate outreach at the center of the platform in a way Ashby's CRM layer doesn't match. Recruiters build multi-step sequences, track who opens and replies, set exit rules, and keep a candidate warm across months before a role opens. Every touchpoint logs on the candidate's record, so the conversation history is complete when the right role finally does open.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby bundles interview scheduling, a full analytics suite, and CRM into one platform, all included on every plan. Lever offers no native scheduling and no comparable analytics, and sells AI Screening as a paid add-on rather than including it. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier. Lever's pricing page shows no numbers at all. Lever also lacks an HR suite, assessments, and native video interviews, so teams that need those capabilities need separate tools.

### Best fit

**Choose Lever if** you have recruiters who will use the CRM and run nurture campaigns daily. Teams that fill roles entirely from inbound applications will find most of Lever's features sit idle.

## 4. Workable: Best for companies that want sourcing and HR from one vendor

[Workable](https://www.workable.com/) is an ATS with a sourcing database, an HR suite, and published pricing. It's built for a company that wants recruiting and HR administration from one platform and is large enough that headcount-based pricing is predictable.

### Why consider it

You want sourcing, recruiting, and HR administration from one vendor. Workable's sourcing database has more than 400 million profiles, and AI sourcing is built on top. Its HR suite covers employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation, so one vendor handles both sides of the hire.

### Key features

* **Sourcing database.** Over 400 million candidate profiles with AI sourcing built in, letting a recruiting team run outbound search from inside the ATS without a separate tool.
* **HR suite.** Employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation sit alongside the ATS in one platform, so recruiting and HR share the same system.
* **Video interviews and assessments.** Premier and Enterprise include native video interviews and candidate assessments; Standard users pay for them as add-ons.
* **Texting.** Two-way candidate texting is a paid add-on on Standard and is included from Premier onward.
* **Generative AI.** Workable Agent is a paid add-on on every plan. It evaluates candidates against an ideal profile, sources passive candidates, and handles candidate chat. It runs on credits starting at $600 for 5,000.

### Pricing

Workable publishes pricing by total company headcount. Standard is $299 per month billed annually for 1–20 employees. Premier is $599 and Enterprise is $719 at the same headcount tier. Standard is also available month to month at $360 per month. At 21–50 employees those prices rise to $419, $829, and $989. Workable HR is a separate add-on at $99 per month on annual billing for 1–20 employees.

### Where it beats Ashby

Workable offers a sourcing database with more than 400 million profiles and an HR suite covering employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation — neither of which Ashby provides. A company that wants recruiting and HR in one contract will find that Workable covers both sides of the employment lifecycle — employee records, onboarding, time tracking, and payroll preparation alongside recruiting — while Ashby covers recruiting alone.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby's analytics are built in across all plans and cover funnel, pipeline, and source reporting with no setup required. Workable Agent is a paid add-on on every plan and requires building an ideal candidate profile per job before reviewing applicants. Ashby's AI is included in its AI credits and works from the job description. Ashby includes native scheduling. Workable depends on integrations for it. Standard plan users who want video interviews and assessments pay extra for them.

### Best fit

**Choose Workable if** your company wants sourcing, recruiting, and HR administration from one vendor and your headcount is steady enough that headcount-based pricing works in your favour. ## 5. Rippling: Best for hiring inside an HR and payroll platform

[Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/) is a workforce management platform. HR, payroll, benefits, device management, and app provisioning are the product, and the recruiting module is one part of it — available only to companies already on the Rippling platform.

### Why consider it

You are already on Rippling for HR and payroll, or you want to run the full employee lifecycle — from job post through payroll — in one system. The candidate record converts to an employee record at hire, and the new hire's payroll, benefits, and device provisioning flow from the same system without re-entry.

### Key features

* **Unified workforce platform.** HR, payroll, benefits, device management, and recruiting share one employee record, so a hire moves from offer to onboarded with no data re-entered across systems.
* **Requisition and offer management.** Hiring managers submit requisitions, and the platform generates and approves offers, with the final offer letter flowing straight into the employee record.
* **Interview scheduling.** The recruiting module handles scheduling, coordinating interviewer availability and sending candidates calendar invites, with no separate tool required.
* **Role-based permissions.** Access is scoped by module. A hiring manager sees only their own roles and candidates. An HR admin sees everything.
* **App provisioning on hire.** When a candidate is hired, Rippling can automatically provision the accounts, apps, and devices the role requires, based on the department and role configuration.

### Pricing

Rippling doesn't publish pricing for the recruiting module. The core Unity Platform starts at a reported $8 per employee per month. The recruiting module is an add-on, quoted separately. Independent sources report roughly $4 per user per month, with total recruiting costs between $150 and $600 per month for most companies. Pricing details require a sales call. Rippling offers no self-serve trial for the recruiting module.

### Where it beats Ashby

Rippling ties recruiting directly to payroll, benefits, and device provisioning — Ashby has no equivalent. When a candidate is hired, the downstream HR and IT setup starts automatically from the same platform. For a company that runs its entire workforce on Rippling, adding the recruiting module avoids a separate ATS integration entirely.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby is a purpose-built recruiting platform with a deep sourcing CRM, scheduling, and analytics that the Rippling recruiting module doesn't match. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier and can be evaluated independently. Rippling's recruiting module requires a sales call and cannot be purchased as a standalone product. The module serves the HR team; it was not built for teams where hiring managers run their own searches.

### Best fit

**Choose Rippling if** your company already runs HR, payroll, and benefits on Rippling and wants recruiting inside the same system. Teams that want a standalone ATS they can evaluate and start using today will find the recruiting module falls outside that scope.

## 6. Teamtailor: Best for employer branding-first recruiting

[Teamtailor](https://www.teamtailor.com/) is an ATS built around the careers site. It focuses on employer branding, candidate experience, and DEI tooling, with applicant tracking built inside that framework.

### Why consider it

Your company's careers site is the primary way candidates find you, and you want the ATS vendor to own the whole experience. Teamtailor's careers site builder is drag-and-drop, with no coding required, and it supports video content, employee testimonials, and culture pages alongside the job listings.

### Key features

* **Careers site builder.** A drag-and-drop editor builds careers pages with video, employee stories, culture sections, and job listings without a developer or a separate CMS. The candidate experience, from first visit to application, runs through a single platform.
* **Anonymous hiring.** Candidate names and photos are hidden from the pipeline until a recruiter chooses to reveal them, reducing early-stage bias in the review process.
* **DEI tools.** EEO surveys, anonymous application review, and diversity reporting are built into the platform for teams that track representation across the funnel.
* **Chrome sourcing extension.** Recruiters add profiles from LinkedIn and other sites directly into the candidate database without navigating away.
* **Generative AI.** AI Co-pilot, available on higher tiers, drafts job descriptions, generates interview questions, and summarizes candidate profiles. A candidate chatbot on the careers site handles applicant questions automatically.

### Pricing

Teamtailor requires a sales conversation for any pricing information. All plans are priced on quote. Third-party sources report Starter at roughly $300 to $400 per month, Growth at $600 to $1,200, and Enterprise at $1,500 or more, with mid-market annual contracts commonly reaching $6,000 to $12,000.

### Where it beats Ashby

Teamtailor's careers site builder is the product's center of gravity in a way Ashby's job board is not. Teams build multi-page careers sites with culture content, video, and employee stories without touching code. Anonymous hiring and EEO reporting ship as core platform features, ready for teams that make DEI a formal part of the screening process.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby's analytics are built in across all plans, but Teamtailor's reporting is lighter on lower tiers. Ashby's scheduling, CRM, and sourcing are part of the core platform, but Teamtailor's sourcing is a Chrome extension with no native CRM layer. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier, but Teamtailor requires a sales conversation for any pricing information. Teamtailor's AI Co-pilot is gated to higher tiers, leaving smaller plans with no AI assistance in the pipeline.

### Best fit

**Choose Teamtailor if** your careers site is the primary channel for attracting candidates and your team wants to own that experience end to end without a developer. If deep recruiting analytics and CRM matter more than branding, Ashby is the stronger fit.

## 7. Pinpoint: Best for mid-market in-house recruiting teams

[Pinpoint](https://www.pinpointhq.com/) is an in-house recruiting platform for companies running several hiring streams at once — desk-based and frontline, early-career and executive, agency-sourced and direct — all in one system.

### Why consider it

You're a mid-market company with multiple hiring streams running at once — agency-sourced roles, employee referrals, and direct applications. Pinpoint covers all three, with an agency portal, a referral program, an onboarding module, and approval chains keyed to salary band.

### Key features

* **Lifecycle automation.** If-this-then-that rules across the candidate lifecycle auto-advance on an assessment score, send templated communications at each step, and route an offer through the approval chain for its salary band.
* **Agency portal.** External recruiters get their own login to submit candidates into the pipeline, see where each one stands, and track fees, without needing an internal seat.
* **Onboarding module.** Hires move from the offer stage into an onboarding module with task lists, document collection, and start-date reminders for the new hire and the manager.
* **Background and reference checks.** A recruiter orders both from the candidate's record, tracks them to completion inside the platform, and receives results attached to the candidate.
* **Generative AI.** Available on the Enterprise tier, Pinpoint includes a hiring copilot that answers plain-language questions and drafts communications, a notetaker that transcribes interviews, a candidate chatbot on the careers site, and candidate match scores against criteria a recruiter sets.

### Pricing

Pinpoint requires a sales conversation for any pricing information. Contracts are annual, and Pinpoint offers no free trial. Third-party reviewers report Growth at roughly $345 per month on annual billing and Enterprise at $1,200 or more, with annual costs estimated between $4,600 and $15,000. AI features, blind screening, and API access are Enterprise-only.

### Where it beats Ashby

Pinpoint has an agency portal and an employee referral program that Ashby doesn't. External recruiters submit candidates through their own login, and employees refer through a tracked program. Lifecycle automation moves candidates on assessment scores and routes each offer for approval by salary band. Background checks, reference checks, and video interviews are included in the platform without a separate purchase.

### Where Ashby wins

Ashby has a sourcing database and CRM built for outbound recruiting. Pinpoint tracks the applicants who come to you and has no outbound sourcing layer. Ashby's analytics are built in across all plans, while Pinpoint gates AI and advanced analytics to Enterprise. Ashby publishes prices for its Foundations tier, while Pinpoint requires a sales conversation for any pricing information. On the Growth tier, Pinpoint offers no AI assistance in the hiring pipeline.

### Best fit

**Choose Pinpoint if** you're a mid-market in-house recruiting team managing multiple hiring streams, working with agencies, and prepared to sign an annual contract. Pinpoint is built for teams running a heavy hiring load and is likely more than a small team filling a handful of roles at a time needs.

## How to choose an Ashby alternative

Work through these before you book any demos. Most teams can rule out half the list in ten minutes.

* **Recruiting team structure.** Do dedicated recruiters own hiring, or do hiring managers run it themselves? Ashby, Greenhouse, and Lever assume a talent acquisition operation. Polymer assumes the hiring manager is doing the hiring alongside a founder or a small team.
* **Need for sourcing or a recruiting CRM.** If outbound sourcing is part of a recruiter's daily work, Lever, Ashby, or Workable has the tooling for it. If you hire from inbound applications, most of that tooling goes unused.
* **Hiring volume and concurrent roles.** How many jobs are open at once, and how many applications does each one get? Polymer's plans are sized by published jobs (5, 20, or 50). Ashby's plans are sized by headcount. Pinpoint and Teamtailor both require annual contracts sized for a mid-market operation.
* **Workflow complexity and flexibility.** Do you need approval chains by salary band, multi-stream pipelines, and an agency portal? Or custom hiring stages and application forms per job? Pinpoint is the former. Polymer is the latter. For [talent acquisition teams](/blog/talent-acquisition-vs-recruitment) running structured processes, Greenhouse or Ashby fits better.
* **Implementation tolerance.** Greenhouse requires a multi-week implementation. Ashby's Foundations tier is self-serve but puts a full platform in front of you to configure. Polymer is ready to use the day you sign up.
* **AI requirements.** Decide whether you want AI screening included, as an add-on, or not at all. Polymer includes AI screening on every plan with no configuration required. Ashby includes AI credits on every plan. Workable sells AI separately, Pinpoint gates it to Enterprise, and Rippling has no recruiting-focused AI.
* **Integration and HR needs.** If you want recruiting inside your HR and payroll system, Rippling is worth evaluating. If you need a wide integration marketplace, Greenhouse has the most partners. Polymer's API, available on every plan, manages candidates and job data.
* **Budget and pricing model.** Ask how the price moves as your company grows. Headcount-based pricing (Ashby, Greenhouse, Workable) rises with the company. Job-based pricing (Polymer) rises with hiring volume. Flat annual contracts (Pinpoint) are predictable but require a commitment. Published pricing (Polymer, Workable) lets you evaluate without a sales call.

## Which Ashby alternative is right for you?

Not every recruiting tool fits every team. Each product on this list targets a different kind of team — lean and intuitive on one end, deep and process-heavy on the other. The right fit depends on how many open roles you run at once, how many people touch each hire, and how formal your process needs to be. Smaller teams often find that built-in process slows adoption rather than helping it. Larger recruiting operations need that structure to keep hiring consistent.
